# Formalization plan — rank-$r$ partial-trace inequality

Target: `paper/rank-r.tex`, "A Sharp Rank-$r$ Partial-Trace Inequality via Double-Skew
Singular-Value Bounds" (July 26, 2026).

## 0. Why this is worth doing, and what that implies about ordering

The manuscript's own disclosure says the arguments were generated by GPT-5.6, that
the author did not independently derive them, and that no expert has checked them.
Its central import, Fu–Gao–Park [arXiv:2607.21367], was posted **three days before**
the manuscript and is itself unreviewed.

So formalization here is not a victory lap over a known-good proof — it is a
**measuring instrument**. That changes the ordering rule:

> Do not work in order of the paper. Work in order of *where a wrong step would
> hide*, cheapest-to-check first.

Everything below is organized by **delicacy**, not by size. Size is not the
constraint: mechanical index-pushing is grindable and the tier tables say so
explicitly. The constraints are (a) steps where the mathematics may actually be
wrong, and (b) steps needing a theorem that does not exist in any Lean library,
which no amount of grinding produces.

## 3. Delicacy tiers

### D0 — Convention risk. *Mechanical to write, highest error density.*

The paper mixes conventions that are individually standard but jointly easy to
get wrong. All are already written down in `RankR/Conventions.lean`.

| Item | tex | Why it bites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ conjugate-linear in **first** arg | §1 | Matches Mathlib; a flip swaps $\mathrm{Tr}\,C \leftrightarrow \overline{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$ throughout §3 |
| $\mathrm{vec}(\lvert x\rangle\langle y\rvert) = x \otimes \bar y$ | §1 | Fixes whether `vec` is entrywise-identity or entrywise-conjugate |
| $\mathrm{Tr}_U$ = trace **over** $U$ | §1 | Thm 1.1 is symmetric so an error is invisible there — it surfaces only in Thm 5.9 (asymmetric) |
| $\mathfrak{so}$ uses $L^T = -L$, not $L^H = -L$ | §2 | The whole `eq:T-orthogonal` argument needs $K^T = K$ |
| The regrouping $(A_1A_2):(B_1B_2)$ | §5 | Every §5–§7 statement is *about* this cut |
| Euclidean vs. sup norm on `EuclideanSpace ℂ (m × n)` | — | Lean-specific: Mathlib's default `Pi` norm is sup; using it typechecks and is wrong |

**Do this first. It is nearly free and it is where the payoff density is highest.**

### D2 — Delicate and self-contained. *This is where a real error would be.*

No external input needed; the argument either works or it doesn't. **Rank-ordered
by my estimate of where a defect is most likely.**

1. **The constant chain `eq:T-by-vertices` → `eq:T-Cauchy` → `eq:T-operator-bound`.**
   Four constants must conspire exactly: the $1/\sqrt2$ from $\eta_{ij}$'s
   normalization, the vertex degree $r-1$, the $1/2$ from Lemma 2.1, and
   $\lVert K^{(ij)}\rVert_2^2 = 4\lVert c^{(ij)}\rVert_2^2$ (from
   $\langle L_\alpha, L_{\alpha'}\rangle = 2\delta$). The product must be
   *exactly* $(r-1)\lVert c\rVert_2^2$ — not $\le$ something larger, or Prop 2.2
   fails and with it the whole paper. It does check by hand
   ($\frac{r-1}{2}\cdot\frac12\cdot 4 = r-1$), but this is precisely the chain
   Lean verifies for free once the pieces are stated, and precisely the kind of
   thing an LLM gets right four times out of five.

2. **`eq:Phi-expansion`**, the four-term expansion of
   $\Phi = \Lambda_U \otimes \Lambda_V \otimes \mathrm{id}_Q$, and its
   specialization `eq:Phi-rhoT` at $Y = \rho^{T_{UV}}$. Signs, and which factor
   carries which partial transpose. The paper's own caveat — "operators are
   placed on their labeled tensor factors, regardless of whether those factors
   are adjacent in the displayed tensor ordering" — is exactly the informality
   Lean will not accept. **Budget real effort for explicit reindexing
   equivalences here**; this is the one place where formalizing costs
   substantially more than reading.

3. **$\Phi(P_-) = \mathcal{T}\mathcal{T}^*$** (`eq:Phi-Pminus-TTstar`). Justified
   by "the vectors in the first line are exactly the columns of $\mathcal T$
   indexed by $(i,j,\alpha,\beta)$". Needs an actual index bijection.

4. **`eq:sos-complete-graph`** (Prop 4.3) claims an **identity**, not a bound.
   The vertex-variance identity is true at degree $r-1$; the risk is the
   prefactor bookkeeping in "adding and subtracting the total edge action gives
   exactly".

5. **`eq:T-orthogonal`**, $\langle\psi, \mathcal Tc\rangle = 0$. Short but
   load-bearing: it is the only thing upgrading $\mathcal T\mathcal T^* \preceq
   (r-1)I$ to $\preceq (r-1)(I - \rho)$, and without the upgrade Thm 1.1 loses
   the $\frac1r\lvert\mathrm{Tr}\,C\rvert^2$ term.

6. **`eq:kronecker-CS`** (Cor 8.1). A Cauchy–Schwarz producing the specific
   combination $\lVert\mathrm{Tr}_1C\rVert_2^2 + \lVert\mathrm{Tr}_2C\rVert_2^2
   - \frac2d\lvert\mathrm{Tr}\,C\rvert^2$ — the $2/d$ comes from tracelessness
   and is easy to misplace.

7. **Thm 7.5 / Thm 7.6** (`thm:mfold-regimes`, `thm:mfold-diagonal-threshold`),
   the heaviest combinatorics in the paper. Sign analysis of the odd-subset
   polynomial $\vartheta_m(t)$. Lowest priority because nothing else depends on
   them, highest per-item risk.

### D3 — External index-matching. *Cheap to check, expensive to get wrong.*

**Lemma 2.1's application of FGP Thm 2.4.** FGP states
$\langle\psi\lvert(\Pi_{\mathcal A}^{(13)} \otimes \Pi_{\mathcal A}^{(24)})\rvert\psi\rangle \le 1/2$
for $SR(\psi) \le 2$ on $(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes 2}_{12} \otimes (\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes 2}_{34}$,
i.e. antisymmetric pairs $(1,3)$ and $(2,4)$ with the Schmidt cut $12:34$. The
paper uses antisymmetric pairs $(U_{\rm out}, U_{\rm in})$, $(V_{\rm out}, V_{\rm in})$
with cut $(U_{\rm out}V_{\rm out}):(U_{\rm in}V_{\rm in})$. **These must be the same
statement under relabeling.** Also in scope: the zero-extension of $U, V$ into a
common $\mathbb C^d$ preserving nonzero singular values and landing in
$\mathfrak{so}(\mathbb C^d) \otimes \mathfrak{so}(\mathbb C^d)$.

If there is an error anywhere in this paper, my bet is here. **Check it
numerically before touching Lean** (see §5).

### D4 — Library-bottleneck. *Grinding does not help; the theorem must exist.*

| Needed | Status | Tractable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ky Fan max principle** for $\sum_{i\le k} s_i^2$ | Absent everywhere | Yes — standard, follows from Mathlib's spectral theorem + Rayleigh. **PR-worthy.** |
| **Best Schmidt-rank-$k$ approximation** (Johnston–Kribs Thm 3.3 = Eckart–Young in Schmidt-coefficient form), `eq:best-SR2-approximation` | Absent | Yes — standard. **PR-worthy.** |
| **Schmidt decomposition** of a bipartite vector | Absent from Mathlib *and* Physlib | Yes, given SVD. Prerequisite for everything in §5–§7. **PR-worthy.** |
| **SVD existence** for complex matrices | Mathlib has `singularValues` but not the factorization | Yes. **PR-worthy.** |
| $\sum_i s_i^2 = \lVert C\rVert_2^2$, Frobenius `InnerProductSpace` | Absent | Yes, easy |
| Schmidt number, block positivity, $r$-positivity + Choi correspondence | Absent | Yes — definitional work plus Physlib's Choi layer |
| Ky Fan **duality**, Frobenius-dual form `eq:ky-fan-duality` | Absent | Yes, given Ky Fan |
| **FGP Thm 2.4** — Takagi factorization (absent from Mathlib) + minimax + first/second variation | Absent | **No.** A separate research-scale project. |

**Decision: carry FGP as an explicit hypothesis, not an `axiom`.** State
`theorem rank_r_partial_trace (hFGP : DoubleSkewBound U V) : …`. Then
`#print axioms` stays a meaningful hygiene check, and the dependency on an
unreviewed three-day-old preprint is visible in the type rather than buried.

The five PR-worthy items are the reusable payoff: they are wanted by Mathlib
independently of this paper, and they are the only reason to touch Lean rather
than a CAS for the §5–§7 material.

### Phase A residual — the exact remaining statement

A0–A4 are done (`Skew.lean`, `Kraus.lean`, `Sectors.lean`, `Phi.lean`; no
`sorry`). Composing `HopScaled_eq`, `Ppos_sub_Pneg` and `Phi4_sub` reduces all
of A5–A8 to a single operator inequality:

    s · Φ₄(Pneg)  ⪯  8s(s−1)·I − 8(s−1)·ρ₀

equivalently, in unscaled `Φ = Φ₄/4` and with `2s²H = s·Φ(Ppos) + bracket`,

    bracket := 2s(s−1)·I − 2(s−1)·ρ₀ − s·Φ(Pneg)  ⪰  0

since `Φ₄(Ppos) ⪰ 0` already follows from `qform_Ppos_nonneg` + `qform_Phi4_nonneg`.
Checked by `preflight.py` ("Prop 2.2 residual"), seven `(dU,dV,s)` triples
including `s = 1`, where `Pneg = 0` and the bracket vanishes identically.

The bracket is singular in every case tested, but that is **structural, not
tightness**: `δ_e` is in its kernel because every column of the synthesis map is
orthogonal to `δ_e` (A7) while `ρ₀ δ_e = s·δ_e`. On `δ_e^⊥` the bracket's
positivity is *equivalent* to the frame bound

    ‖W‖² ≤ 2(s−1),     W the matrix with columns  placeQ (L_α ⊗ M_β) ζᵢⱼ

which is attained at `(dU,dV,s) = (2,2,2)` (`‖W‖² = 2.000000`) and has slack
elsewhere — e.g. `2.70` against a target of `4` at `(3,2,3)`. So A6 does have
room, unlike D2 #1. The SOS route is still preferable, but for grindability
rather than because the margin is zero.

A route that avoids constructing `𝒯` as a matrix at all: by `qform_krausSum`
and `qform_rankOne`,

    qform (Φ₄ Pneg) x = ∑_{a,b,c,d} ∑_{i<j} ‖⟪ζᵢⱼ, (placeQ (L⊗M))ᴴ x⟫‖²
                      = ∑_{a,b,c,d} ∑_{i<j} ‖⟪(placeQ (L⊗M)) ζᵢⱼ, x⟫‖²

so A5 becomes an adjoint-move plus `qform_rankOne`, and A7 becomes
`⟪δ_e, placeQ N ζᵢⱼ⟫ = 0` for symmetric `N` — which follows from
`transpose_eq_self_of_mem_doubleSkew` ∘ `kron_mem_doubleSkew`, both already
proved in `Skew.lean`. All four of `Φ(Pneg) = W Wᴴ`, `‖W‖² ≤ 2(s−1)`,
`W ⊥ δ_e`, and the SOS identity below are numerically verified in exactly these
conventions.

**Revision to A6: the SOS identity is not needed.** The roadmap preferred it
over `eq:T-Cauchy` on the grounds that the constant chain is slackless. With the
*unordered* Kraus family already used in `Phi.lean` that is no longer true: the
step `‖K‖₂² ≤ 16‖c‖²` is lossy (max observed ratio `4.97` against the bound `16`,
because `{L_ab ⊗ M_cd}` over all ordered pairs is not an orthogonal family). So
the plain Cauchy–Schwarz route closes with room to spare, and it is four short
steps instead of a large identity:

    ‖𝒯c‖²  =  ∑ₖ ‖uₖ‖²                                  (eq:T-by-vertices, `norm_delta`)
           ≤  (s−1) ∑ₖ ∑_{e ∋ k} ‖v_{k,e}‖²             (Cauchy–Schwarz, s−1 terms)
           =  (s−1) ∑_{i<j} (‖K_ij ēᵢ‖² + ‖K_ij ēⱼ‖²)   (regroup by edge)
           ≤  (s−1) ∑_{i<j} ½‖K_ij‖₂²                   (`DoubleSkewBound` edgewise)
           ≤  8(s−1) ‖c‖²                               (‖K‖₂² ≤ 16‖c‖²)

Note also that this route needs **no basis and no linear order** on `U` or `V`:
the vertex regrouping is basis-free, and the edgewise step consumes
`DoubleSkewBound` directly. Verified at six `(dU,dV,s)` triples.

The manuscript's SOS identity, in this normalization (`ζᵢⱼ` unnormalized,
`‖L_ab‖₂² = 2`), is `eq:sos-complete-graph` doubled — kept here for reference,
and still the right thing to prove if Prop 4.3 is wanted for its own sake
(Phase C):

    2(s−1)‖c‖² − ‖𝒯c‖²
      = (s−1) ∑_{i<j} [ ½‖K_ij‖₂² − ‖K_ij ēᵢ‖² − ‖K_ij ēⱼ‖² ]
        + ∑_k ∑_{e<f incident to k} ‖v_{k,e} − v_{k,f}‖²

with `K_ij = ∑_{α,β} c^{(ij)}_{αβ} L_α ⊗ M_β`, and `v_{k,e}` as in the
manuscript. The edge brackets are exactly `DoubleSkewBound` applied to
`K_ij ∈ doubleSkew` (via `sum_smul_kron_mem_doubleSkew`) with the orthonormal
pair `ēᵢ, ēⱼ` (via `orthonormal_conj`); the vertex terms are manifestly ≥ 0.

Going from `‖𝒯c‖² ≤ 2(s−1)‖c‖²` to the operator form `W Wᴴ ⪯ 2(s−1)·I` is the
square-root-free step: `‖Wᴴy‖⁴ = |⟪W(Wᴴy), y⟫|² ≤ ‖W(Wᴴy)‖²‖y‖² ≤ 2(s−1)‖Wᴴy‖²‖y‖²`,
then cancel.

### Phase B — shrink the trusted surface to FGP's literal statement

**Revised: no SVD, no Ky Fan, no best-rank-2 approximation.** The manuscript
routes through singular values — `s₁² + s₂² ≤ ½‖K‖₂²` via the best-rank-2
approximation identity, then Ky Fan to get the action bound. That route needs
complex SVD existence, which PLAN.md called "the one big rock". It can be
skipped entirely.

With `P := |x⟩⟨x| + |y⟩⟨y|` for the orthonormal pair `x, y`:

    ‖Kx‖² + ‖Ky‖²  =  ‖KP‖₂²        and     rank (KP) ≤ 2
    ⟪vec K, vec (KP)⟫  =  ⟪K, KP⟫_HS  =  ‖KP‖₂²
    Q₋ (vec K) = vec K,  Q₋ a self-adjoint projection, so with A := ‖KP‖₂²,
    A = ⟪vec K, Q₋ vec (KP)⟫ ≤ ‖K‖₂ · ‖Q₋ vec (KP)‖,
    ‖Q₋ vec (KP)‖² = ⟪vec (KP), Q₋ vec (KP)⟫ ≤ ½A        [FGP, rank ≤ 2]
    ⟹ A² ≤ ‖K‖₂² · ½A ⟹ A ≤ ½‖K‖₂².

FGP is applied directly to `vec (KP)`, never to an optimizer, so no
approximation theorem is needed; the last step is the same square-root-free
cancellation used in `frame_le_of_synthesis_le`. Verified at `d = 2,3,4`.

**The index matching (old B1) is a type-level identification, not a
permutation.** For `K : Matrix (U × V) (U × V) ℂ` the vectorization is indexed
by `(U × V) × (U × V)`, i.e. `(row_U, row_V, col_U, col_V)`. Reading those four
slots as factors `1,2,3,4` puts `U_out = 1`, `V_out = 2`, `U_in = 3`,
`V_in = 4` — so the antisymmetrized pairs *are* FGP's `(1,3)` and `(2,4)`, and
the row:col cut *is* FGP's `12:34`, by construction of `vec`. What has to be
proved is only that `Q₋` fixes `vec K` for `K ∈ doubleSkew`.

**One definitional choice to flag.** FGP quantifies over pure states of Schmidt
rank ≤ 2 across `12:34`. Stating it instead over matrices `M` with
`rank M ≤ 2`, via `ψ = vec M`, avoids defining Schmidt rank at all. The
identification `SR(vec M) = rank M` is standard, but it is a *rendering* of
FGP's statement, not a theorem proved here — exactly the kind of thing the
README says Lean cannot certify.

Steps:

- **B1 — `Antisym.lean`.** The two factor swaps, `Q₋` entrywise, `Q₋` is a
  self-adjoint idempotent, and `Q₋ (vec K) = vec K` for `K ∈ doubleSkew`.
- **B2 — `FGP.lean`.** State `FGPBound`, prove `FGPBound U V → DoubleSkewBound U V`
  by the chain above.
- **B3 — unequal dimensions.** FGP is stated on `(ℂ^d)^{⊗4}`. Zero-extension of
  `U, V` into a common `ℂ^d` preserves skewness and Hilbert–Schmidt norm; this
  is the only remaining piece, and it is reindexing.

*Artifact:* `rank_r_partial_trace (hFGP : FGPBound d)` — the hypothesis must
carry the **square** form, `FGPBound d` on `(ℂ^d)^{⊗4}` with all four factors
the same space, which is what Fu–Gao–Park actually publish. A `U, V`-indexed
`FGPBound U V` is a *generalization* of their theorem; it is fine as an
intermediate (B3 derives it from the square form by zero-extension) but must
not be the artifact's hypothesis, or the trusted surface becomes "FGP
generalized" rather than "FGP as published" — the distinction this project
exists to maintain.

### Ordering rationale and stop points

**A then B1, before anything else.** A closes the paper's own argument; B1
attacks the one step most likely to hide a real error, and it is cheap.
Everything after that is coverage, not risk reduction. Stopping after A
verifies the theorem modulo Lemma 2.1; stopping after B modulo FGP's
published statement.

### Delegation

A1–A4 and C are grindable — hand to the `lean4` sorry-filler/proof-repair
agents. Keep A5, A6, and all of B1 in the main loop: they are where fidelity
to the paper is the point, not proof search.

## 6. Ground rules

- **Statements before proofs.** Every milestone starts by writing all its
  statements with `sorry`, then filling them. Statements are where the value is;
  proofs are where the grinding is.
- **FGP is a hypothesis, never an `axiom`.** Same for anything else imported
  from an unverified source.
- **`Matrix`, not abstract `TensorProduct`.** The paper fixes bases anyway
  (transposition, $\mathfrak{so}$, vectorization), Physlib's partial trace is
  matrix-level, and `Matrix (U × V) (U × V) ℂ` reindexes cheaply. Mathlib's
  `TensorProduct.instInnerProductSpace` stays available if an abstract argument
  turns out to be cleaner.
- **Reindexing is not free.** The paper's "operators are placed on their labeled
  tensor factors" needs explicit `Equiv`s. Build a small reindexing API early
  (M0/M1) rather than fighting it inside D2 proofs.
- **Record every discrepancy**, including ones that turn out to be harmless
  typos. The output of this project is a list of what the paper got wrong, not
  just a `.olean`.
