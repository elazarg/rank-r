\begin{abstract}
We prove a \emph{pair-amplification} theorem: one number computed at
level two controls $r$-positivity at every level $r$.  To a completely
positive map $\Phi$ admitting a Kraus representation with $A_a^T=A_a$,
attach $\beta_2(\Phi)=\frac12\norm{J(\Phi)}_{S(2)}$, the rank-two
operator norm of its Choi operator.  Then
$\Phi\circ\tau+(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\bigl(\Delta-\frac1r\id\bigr)$ is
$r$-positive for every $r$, where $\Delta(X)=\Tr(X)I$ and $\tau$ is
transposition.  Two mechanisms drive it: an ancilla construction and a
partial transpose reduce the assertion to pair-indexed antisymmetric
modes, over which a complete-graph summation costs only the vertex
degree $r-1$; and transpose symmetry --- a $\Z/2$-degree condition on
the Kraus space --- protects one distinguished direction, which sharpens
the trace coefficient from $1$ to $1/r$.

Its principal corollary, at $\Phi=\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$ together
with a contraction identity for that map, is the arbitrary-matrix
rank-$r$ partial-trace inequality: for every operator $C$ of rank at
most $r$ on a finite-dimensional bipartite space,
\[
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2
    \le r\norm{C}_2^2+\frac{1}{r}\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\]
Both coefficients are optimal --- the first outright, the second once
the first is fixed --- whenever one factor has dimension at least $r$
and the other at least $2$, and for nonzero $C$ equality forces
$\rank C=r$.  This is the extension beyond normal matrices that Costa
Rico and Wolf identify as open; it was obtained independently and
concurrently by Fraser, Huber, Pozsgay and Vona, by a different route.

There $\beta_2=1$ is a two-vector action bound on the double-skew space
$\mathfrak{so}(U)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(V)$, proved here from the
Autonne--Takagi factorization and equivalent to Fu, Gao, and Park's
sharp Schmidt-rank-two projection estimate, and the amplified map is
$r\,\mathcal R_{-1/r}\otimes\mathcal R_{-1/r}$ for
$\mathcal R_\alpha(X)=\Tr(X)I+\alpha X$ --- an identity that fixes
the meaning of both constants: $r-1$ is the vertex degree, and $1/r$ is
the only coefficient a unitarily covariant correction is free to carry.

The development is machine-checked in Lean~4 in full, proved end to
end.  What is certified is the deduction, not the faithfulness of
the formal definitions to their informal counterparts;
\cref{app:formal} gives the repository, the versions, the reported
axioms, and the formal--informal correspondence.

Consequences include the exact rank-constrained two-copy score curve,
an exact asymmetric tensor-product score theorem with the resulting
$r$-positivity classification, Kronecker-sum Ky Fan bounds, and
Schmidt-number witnesses.  These applications are developed in prose
only; they are not part of the machine-checked development.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Costa Rico proved the rank-sensitive partial-trace inequality below
for normal operators and related its unrestricted rank-two case to
two-copy distillability of Werner states \cite{CostaRico}. Costa Rico
and Wolf subsequently developed partial-trace relations for general
matrices, including singular-value majorization results for Kronecker
sums and applications to Schmidt-number witnesses and positive maps,
while identifying the extension of the same sharp rank-sensitive bound
beyond normal matrices as an open problem \cite{CostaRicoWolf}. Fu,
Gao, and Park recently resolved the rank-two endpoint through a sharp
estimate for the $S(2)$-norm of a double-antisymmetric projection
\cite{fu2026solution2copydistillabilitywerner}. Their estimate
supplies the sharp local rank-two input, but not by itself a mechanism
for combining such information across an $r$-dimensional range.

The argument below supplies that mechanism and obtains the
arbitrary-matrix inequality for every rank, unconditionally.  The
rank-two input is \cref{lem:double-skew}, a two-vector action bound on
the double-skew space $\mathfrak{so}(U)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(V)$, which
\cref{sec:double-skew} proves from the Autonne--Takagi factorization by
the argument of
\cite[\S2]{fu2026solution2copydistillabilitywerner}; the reduction
across an $r$-dimensional range is new.  An $r$-dimensional ancilla recording an
orthonormal basis of the range of $C$, a partial transpose, and a
completely positive map then represent the potentially negative
sector by antisymmetric modes indexed by pairs $1\le i<j\le r$, each
controlled by that bound. Grouping the modes by the vertices of the
complete graph $K_r$, rather than estimating its $\binom r2$ edges
independently, costs only the vertex degree $r-1$; a cancellation in
the distinguished code direction then improves the coefficient of
$\abs{\Tr C}^2$ from $1$ to the sharp $1/r$.

That mechanism never looks at the double-skew space, and
\cref{sec:amplification} states it separately as \cref{thm:amplification},
\emph{pair amplification}.  Its inputs are two: a completely positive $\Phi$
whose Kraus operators are transpose-symmetric, and the single number
$\beta_2(\Phi)$ of \cref{def:beta2}.  \Cref{thm:rank_r} is the instance
$\Phi=\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$, where $\beta_2=1$; \cref{rem:scope} records
what the two obvious further instances need. Beyond threshold statements, the applications determine the exact
rank-constrained score curves --- the minimum of the normalized
two-copy filter form over nonzero matrices of rank at most $r$, as in
\cref{eq:exact-q2-score} --- for both symmetric and asymmetric
two-copy filters; the asymmetric formula yields a complete
$r$-positivity classification for the corresponding tensor-product
maps.

The Werner-state and distillability terminology used in the applications
follows the standard conventions \cite{Werner1989,Horodecki1998}.
Throughout, $\norm{\cdot}_2$ denotes the Hilbert--Schmidt, or
Schatten-$2$, norm. The notation $\Tr_U$ means the partial trace
\emph{over} the factor $U$. Inner products are conjugate-linear in the
first argument and linear in the second. Relative to chosen orthonormal
bases, vectorization is normalized by
\[
    \operatorname{vec}(\ketbra{x}{y})
    =x\otimes\overline y.
\]
The symbol $\SR(z)$ denotes the Schmidt rank of a vector $z$, and
$\SN(\sigma)$ denotes the Schmidt number of a positive operator or
state $\sigma$, always across the bipartition indicated in context.
With the above normalization, a vector has Schmidt rank at most $k$
across the output--input cut exactly when it is the vectorization of a
matrix of rank at most $k$; that is,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SR-vec-rank}
    \SR\bigl(\operatorname{vec}(A)\bigr)=\rank A ,
\end{equation}
a fact used repeatedly below.  Finally, for an operator on a twofold
tensor power, $\Tr_1$ denotes the partial trace \emph{over} the first
local factor, leaving an operator on the second, and $\Tr_2$ traces
over the second.

The paper is self-contained modulo one classical ingredient, the
Autonne--Takagi factorization of a complex symmetric matrix
\cite[Cor.~4.4.4]{HornJohnson}, from which \cref{lem:double-skew} is
proved in \cref{sec:double-skew} following the strategy of
\cite[\S2]{fu2026solution2copydistillabilitywerner}; so
\cref{lem:double-skew} is not imported from their work but proved, and
the formal development of \cref{app:formal} proves the Autonne--Takagi
factorization as well, making that chain complete end to end.  \Cref{rem:fgp-equivalence} records that
\cref{lem:double-skew} is \emph{equivalent} to the sharp projection
estimate of Fu, Gao, and Park, so their Theorem~2.4 may be substituted
for the proof given here, and conversely.

The main theorem is the mechanism.  For a completely positive $\Phi$ on
$\cL(H)$ with Choi operator $J(\Phi)=\sum_a\ketbra{\operatorname{vec}A_a}
{\operatorname{vec}A_a}$, write $\beta_2(\Phi):=\frac12\norm{J(\Phi)}_{S(2)}$
for half the supremum of $\ip z{J(\Phi)z}$ over unit vectors of Schmidt rank at
most two (\cref{def:beta2}), and let $\Delta(X):=\Tr(X)I$ and $\tau$ be
transposition.

\begin{theorem}[Pair amplification]
\label[theorem]{thm:pair-amplification}
Let $\Phi$ be completely positive on $\cL(H)$, $\dim H<\infty$, admitting a
Kraus representation with $A_a^T=A_a$.  Then for every $r\ge1$ the map
\[
    \Psi_{r,\Phi}
    :=
    \Phi\circ\tau
    +(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\left(\Delta-\tfrac1r\id\right)
\]
is $r$-positive.
\end{theorem}

One number, computed at level two, controls every level $r$; the cost grows
linearly, with slope $\beta_2(\Phi)$, and vanishes at $r=1$, where the assertion
is that $\Phi\circ\tau$ is positive.  The two hypotheses act separately, and
\cref{thm:amplification} states the operator forms this splits into: complete
positivity and $\beta_2$ give the bound, and transpose symmetry --- a
$\Z/2$-degree condition on the Kraus space, \cref{rem:parity} --- supplies the
$\frac1r\id$, which is exactly the difference between the coefficients $1$ and
$1/r$ below.  \Cref{sec:amplification} proves it.

Its principal corollary is the estimate the introduction began with.  It is not
a special case: passing from $r$-positivity to the marginals needs a contraction
identity for the particular map $\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$
(\cref{eq:Phi-rhoT}), and \cref{rem:constant-dependence} shows that $\beta_2$
alone cannot carry it.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-$r$ partial-trace inequality]
\label[theorem]{thm:rank_r}
Let $U$ and $V$ be finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces. If
$C\in\cL(U\otimes V)$ is nonzero and $r_0:=\rank C$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:main-bound-exact-rank}
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2
    \le r_0\norm{C}_2^2+\frac{1}{r_0}\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $r\in\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ such that $\rank C\le r$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:main-bound}
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2
    \le r\norm{C}_2^2+\frac{1}{r}\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\end{equation}
The assertion is immediate when $C=0$.
\end{theorem}

\Cref{eq:main-bound} was obtained independently and concurrently by Fraser,
Huber, Pozsgay and Vona \cite{FraserHuberPozsgayVona2026}, by a different
route; Bharti, Gajjala and Haug \cite{BhartiGajjalaHaug2026} obtain the
rank-two case together with finite-copy extensions.  Both, like the present
work, resolve the two-copy distillability question through Costa Rico's
equivalence.  The overlap is the inequality; what is particular to this
account is the derivation from \cref{thm:pair-amplification}, together with
the formal development of \cref{app:formal}.

\begin{remark}[Optimality of the coefficients]
\label[remark]{rem:optimality}
The coefficients are optimal whenever $\dim U\ge r$ and $\dim V\ge2$,
or with $U$ and $V$ interchanged. More precisely, the coefficient $r$
of $\norm{C}_2^2$ is optimal, and conditional on choosing this optimal
coefficient, the coefficient $1/r$ of $\abs{\Tr C}^2$ is optimal.

Indeed, choose
\[
    C=P_r\otimes\ketbra{v}{w},
\]
where $P_r$ is a rank-$r$ projection on $U$ and $v,w\in V$ are unit
vectors. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2&=r^2,\\
    \norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2&=r\abs{\ip{w}{v}}^2,\\
    \norm{C}_2^2&=r,\\
    \abs{\Tr C}^2&=r^2\abs{\ip{w}{v}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus equality holds in \cref{eq:main-bound}, and the displayed
extremizer has $\rank C=r$ exactly.  More generally, for nonzero $C$
equality in the rank-$r$ inequality requires $\rank C=r$; the passage
from the exact-rank inequality to a strictly larger rank parameter is
strict.  This is why the sharpness example is built from a rank-$r$
projection. For a bound of the form
\[
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2
    \le a\norm{C}_2^2+b\abs{\Tr C}^2,
\]
taking $v\perp w$ forces $a\ge r$. Once $a=r$ is fixed, taking $v=w$
forces $b\ge1/r$.
\end{remark}

The proof of \cref{thm:rank_r}, and of every result it depends on, has been
formalized in Lean~4, as have the statements of \cref{rem:optimality} and
\cref{lem:one-sided-partial-trace}.
\Cref{app:formal} records the repository, the toolchain and Mathlib pins, the
reported axioms, and the correspondence between the formal and informal
statements.

\section{Double-Skew Estimates and the Marginal Operator Inequality}
\label{sec:double-skew}

We first prove the double-skew action bound used below.  The route is that
of \cite[\S2]{fu2026solution2copydistillabilitywerner}, with the
Autonne--Takagi factorization in place of their Theorem~2.4;
\cref{rem:fgp-equivalence} records that the two are equivalent.

\begin{lemma}[Double-skew action bound]
\label[lemma]{lem:double-skew}
Fix orthonormal bases of $U$ and $V$, and let
\[
    \mathfrak{so}(U)
    :=\{L\in\cL(U):L^T=-L\},
    \qquad
    \mathfrak{so}(V)
    :=\{M\in\cL(V):M^T=-M\}.
\]
For every $K$ in the linear span of
\[
    \{L\otimes M:
      L\in\mathfrak{so}(U),\ M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)\},
\]
and every orthonormal pair $x,y\in U\otimes V$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:double-skew-action}
    \norm{Kx}^2+\norm{Ky}^2
    \le\frac12\norm{K}_2^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Index operators on $U\otimes V$ by pairs relative to the fixed bases,
writing $N_{(a,b),(a',b')}$, and define the two partial transposes
\[
    (N^{T_U})_{(a,b),(a',b')}:=N_{(a',b),(a,b')},
    \qquad
    (N^{T_V})_{(a,b),(a',b')}:=N_{(a,b'),(a',b)},
\]
whose composite is the ordinary transpose.  Both are self-adjoint
involutions for the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product and they commute, so
\[
    \Pi:=\tfrac12\bigl(1-T_U\bigr)\,\tfrac12\bigl(1-T_V\bigr)
\]
is the orthogonal projection of $\cL(U\otimes V)$ onto
$\mathfrak{so}(U)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(V)$.  Hence $\Pi K=K$; and since a
tensor product of two skew-symmetric matrices is symmetric, also
$K^T=K$.

\emph{Step 1: a symmetric Schmidt decomposition.}  Because $K^T=K$, the
Autonne--Takagi factorization \cite[Cor.~4.4.4]{HornJohnson} supplies an
orthonormal basis $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ of $U\otimes V$ and reals $s_i\ge0$
with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:takagi-K}
    K=\sum_i s_i\,w_iw_i^T .
\end{equation}
The $w_iw_i^T$ are themselves orthonormal in the Hilbert--Schmidt inner
product, because
$\ip{uu^T}{vv^T}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\ip uv^2$.

\emph{Step 2: the two-term bound.}  Write $M_u$ for the
$\dim U\times\dim V$ coefficient matrix of $u\in U\otimes V$, so that
$\norm{M_u}_2=\norm u$.  Carrying out the two inner sums separately
gives the flip trace identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flip-trace}
    \ip{uu^T}{(vv^T)^{T_U}}_{\mathrm{HS}}
    =\Tr\bigl((M_u^*M_v)^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
Let $N:=s_1w_1w_1^T+s_2w_2w_2^T$ with $s_1,s_2\ge0$, write $M_i:=M_{w_i}$
and $c_i:=\norm{M_i^*M_i}_2$.  Expanding by \cref{eq:flip-trace}, using
that $M_i^*M_i$ is self-adjoint for the diagonal terms and that the two
cross terms are complex conjugates,
\[
    \ip N{N^{T_U}}_{\mathrm{HS}}
    =s_1^2c_1^2
      +2s_1s_2\,\operatorname{Re}\Tr\bigl((M_1^*M_2)^2\bigr)
      +s_2^2c_2^2 .
\]
Two applications of Cauchy--Schwarz for
$\ip\cdot\cdot_{\mathrm{HS}}$, namely
\[
    \abs{\Tr(X^2)}=\bigl|\ip{X^*}{X}_{\mathrm{HS}}\bigr|\le\norm X_2^2,
    \qquad
    \norm{A^*B}_2^2=\ip{AA^*}{BB^*}_{\mathrm{HS}}
      \le\norm{A^*A}_2\,\norm{B^*B}_2,
\]
the second using $\norm{AA^*}_2=\norm{A^*A}_2$, bound the cross term
below by $-2s_1s_2c_1c_2$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:two-term-nonneg}
    \ip N{N^{T_U}}_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge(s_1c_1-s_2c_2)^2\ge0 .
\end{equation}
Since $N^T=N$ forces $N^{T_V}=N^{T_U}$, expanding $\Pi$ leaves only two
distinct terms, and \cref{eq:two-term-nonneg} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pi-symmetric}
    \ip N{\Pi N}_{\mathrm{HS}}
    =\tfrac12\bigl(\norm N_2^2-\ip N{N^{T_U}}_{\mathrm{HS}}\bigr)
    \le\tfrac12\norm N_2^2 .
\end{equation}
This is the only place the hypothesis $\dim\le2$ of
\cite[Lem.~2.3]{fu2026solution2copydistillabilitywerner} is used, and
\cref{eq:two-term-nonneg} is that lemma.

\emph{Step 3: truncation.}  Fix $a\ne b$ and put
$K_{ab}:=s_aw_aw_a^T+s_bw_bw_b^T$.  Orthonormality of the $w_iw_i^T$ gives
$\norm{K_{ab}}_2^2=\ip K{K_{ab}}_{\mathrm{HS}}=s_a^2+s_b^2=:A$.  As $\Pi$ is a
self-adjoint idempotent with $\Pi K=K$,
\[
    A=\ip K{K_{ab}}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\ip{\Pi K}{K_{ab}}_{\mathrm{HS}}
     =\ip K{\Pi K_{ab}}_{\mathrm{HS}}
     \le\norm{K}_2\,\norm{\Pi K_{ab}}_2,
\]
while $\norm{\Pi K_{ab}}_2^2=\ip{K_{ab}}{\Pi K_{ab}}_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\frac12A$ by
\cref{eq:pi-symmetric}.  Squaring the first display and substituting the
second yields $A^2\le\frac12A\norm{K}_2^2$.  If $A=0$ there is nothing to
prove; otherwise one factor of $A$ cancels, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pair-bound}
    s_a^2+s_b^2\le\tfrac12\norm{K}_2^2
    \qquad\text{for \emph{every} pair }a\ne b .
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 4: from pairs to the action.}  By \cref{eq:takagi-K} and
orthonormality,
$K^*K=\sum_i s_i^2\ketbra{\overline{w_i}}{\overline{w_i}}$, so for an
orthonormal pair $x,y$,
\[
    \norm{Kx}^2+\norm{Ky}^2=\sum_i s_i^2\theta_i,
    \qquad
    \theta_i:=\abs{\ip{\overline{w_i}}x}^2+\abs{\ip{\overline{w_i}}y}^2 .
\]
Each $\theta_i$ equals $\norm{P\overline{w_i}}^2$ for the rank-two
orthogonal projection $P$ onto $\operatorname{span}\{x,y\}$, so
$\theta_i\in[0,1]$; and $\sum_i\theta_i=\Tr P=2$ because
$\{\overline{w_i}\}$ is an orthonormal basis.  For weights of this kind
and any $c_i\ge0$, letting $a,b$ index the two largest $c_i$ and
bounding every remaining term by $c_b\theta_i$,
\[
    \sum_i c_i\theta_i
    \le c_a\theta_a+c_b(2-\theta_a)
    =2c_b+\theta_a(c_a-c_b)
    \le c_a+c_b .
\]
Applying this with $c_i=s_i^2$, and then \cref{eq:pair-bound} to that
pair, gives \cref{eq:double-skew-action}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Equivalence with the Fu--Gao--Park estimate]
\label[remark]{rem:fgp-equivalence}
\Cref{lem:double-skew} is equivalent to
\cite[Thm.~2.4]{fu2026solution2copydistillabilitywerner}, so that
estimate may be substituted for the proof above.

Label the four vectorization factors
$U_{\mathrm{out}},V_{\mathrm{out}},U_{\mathrm{in}},V_{\mathrm{in}}$ as
$1,2,3,4$; then the antisymmetrized pairs
$(U_{\mathrm{out}},U_{\mathrm{in}})$ and
$(V_{\mathrm{out}},V_{\mathrm{in}})$ are Fu--Gao--Park's $(1,3)$ and
$(2,4)$, the cut
$(U_{\mathrm{out}}V_{\mathrm{out}}):(U_{\mathrm{in}}V_{\mathrm{in}})$ is
their $12{:}34$, and $\operatorname{vec}$ intertwines $\Pi$ with
$Q_-:=P_{\wedge^2U}^{(1,3)}\otimes P_{\wedge^2V}^{(2,4)}$.  Their
estimate states that, for every $z$ of Schmidt rank at most two across
that cut,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fgp-bound-homogeneous}
    \ip z{Q_-z}\le\tfrac12\norm z^2 ,
\end{equation}
both sides being homogeneous of degree two, so that no normalization is
needed.  By \cref{eq:SR-vec-rank} this says exactly that
$\ip M{\Pi M}_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\frac12\norm M_2^2$ for every
$M\in\cL(U\otimes V)$ with $\rank M\le2$.

\emph{Their estimate implies \cref{lem:double-skew}.}  Let
$P=\ketbra xx+\ketbra yy$ and $A=\norm{KP}_2^2$.  Since $P^2=P=P^*$,
$\norm{Kx}^2+\norm{Ky}^2=A$ and $\rank(KP)\le2$; and as $\Pi$ is a
self-adjoint idempotent fixing $K$,
$A=\ip K{KP}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\ip K{\Pi(KP)}_{\mathrm{HS}}
\le\norm{K}_2\norm{\Pi(KP)}_2$, while
$\norm{\Pi(KP)}_2^2\le\frac12A$.  Cancelling one factor of $A$ gives
\cref{eq:double-skew-action}.  (A block-zero extension reduces unequal
local dimensions to the square case they state, since extending a
skew-symmetric matrix by zero preserves skew-symmetry and the
Hilbert--Schmidt norm.)

\emph{Conversely.}  Given $M$ with $\rank M\le2$, choose an orthonormal
pair $v_1,v_2$ spanning a subspace that contains the row space
$\operatorname{ran}M^*=(\ker M)^\perp$, and set $u_i:=Mv_i$; then
$M=\sum_{i\le2}\ketbra{u_i}{v_i}$ and
$\norm{M}_2^2=\sum_i\norm{u_i}^2$.  (If $\dim(U\otimes V)=1$ then
$\Pi=0$ and there is nothing to prove.)  Put $K:=\Pi M$.  Then
$\norm{K}_2^2=\ip K M_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sum_i\ip{Kv_i}{u_i}
\le\bigl(\sum_i\norm{Kv_i}^2\bigr)^{1/2}\norm{M}_2
\le\bigl(\tfrac12\norm{K}_2^2\bigr)^{1/2}\norm{M}_2$
by \cref{eq:double-skew-action}, whence
$\ip M{\Pi M}_{\mathrm{HS}}=\norm{\Pi M}_2^2\le\frac12\norm{M}_2^2$.

Both directions are machine-checked; see \cref{app:formal}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Pair amplification}
\label{sec:amplification}

\Cref{lem:double-skew} is one instance of a general mechanism, and it is
cleaner to state the general form first and specialize.  What the argument of
\cref{sec:proof} consumes is a single number attached to a completely positive
map.

\begin{definition}[Rank-two Choi constant]
\label[definition]{def:beta2}
Let $\Phi$ be completely positive on $\cL(H)$ with Kraus family $\{A_a\}$, and
let
\[
    J(\Phi):=\sum_a\ketbra{\operatorname{vec}A_a}{\operatorname{vec}A_a}
\]
be its Choi operator.  Set
\[
    \beta_2(\Phi)
    :=\tfrac12\norm{J(\Phi)}_{S(2)}
    =\tfrac12\sup\bigl\{\ip z{J(\Phi)z}:
      \operatorname{SR}(z)\le2,\ \norm z=1\bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

$J(\Phi)$ is the same operator for every Kraus family of $\Phi$, so
$\beta_2$ depends on $\Phi$ alone --- no minimality and no orthogonality of the
family is required.  It is positively homogeneous in $\Phi$ --- and convex, being
a supremum of linear functionals, but not additive --- so a map and its constant
always carry the same scale and no normalization convention has to be tracked.

Writing $K_\zeta:=\sum_a\zeta_aA_a$ and $s_i$ for singular values, three
descriptions coincide:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:beta2-three-ways}
    2\beta_2(\Phi)
    =\norm{J(\Phi)}_{S(2)}
    =\sup_{\norm\zeta=1}\bigl(s_1(K_\zeta)^2+s_2(K_\zeta)^2\bigr)
    =\sup_{\norm\zeta=1}\bigl\Vert K_\zeta K_\zeta^*\bigr\Vert_{(2)},
\end{equation}
$\norm\cdot_{(k)}$ being the Ky Fan $k$-norm; any of the three may serve as the
definition.  The middle equality is the $S(k)$-norm projection duality of
\cite{JohnstonKribs2010} at $k=2$, and the right-hand quantity is the threshold
characterizing $k$-positivity of $a\Tr(\cdot)I-\Phi$ in
\cite[Thm.~3.2]{MlynikOsakaMarciniak2025}, so $2\beta_2$ is their $a_2$.
By \cref{rem:fgp-equivalence}, $J(\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V)$ is $4Q_-$ and
$\norm{Q_-}_{S(2)}=\frac12$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:beta2-double-skew}
    \beta_2(\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V)=1 .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Pair amplification, operator forms]
\label[theorem]{thm:amplification}
Let $\Phi$ be completely positive on $\cL(U\otimes V)$.  Then, in the notation
of \cref{prop:operator_ineq}, for every $r\ge1$ and every orthonormal family
$e_1,\ldots,e_r$ in $U\otimes V$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:amplification-A0}
    (\Phi\otimes\id_Q)(P_-)
    \preceq
    (r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\,I_{UVQ},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:amplification-B0}
    (\Phi\otimes\id_Q)\bigl(\rho_0^{T_{UV}}\bigr)
    +(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\,I_{UVQ}
    \succeq0 .
\end{equation}
If moreover the Kraus operators of $\Phi$ are \emph{transpose-symmetric},
$A_a^T=A_a$, then $I_{UVQ}$ may be replaced by $I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e$ throughout:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:amplification-A}
    (\Phi\otimes\id_Q)(P_-)
    \preceq
    (r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\,(I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:amplification-B}
    (\Phi\otimes\id_Q)\bigl(\rho_0^{T_{UV}}\bigr)
    +(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\,(I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e)
    \succeq0 .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The two hypotheses act separately, and the gap between the two pairs of displays
is the single direction $\delta_e$.  Complete positivity and $\beta_2$ give
\cref{eq:amplification-A0,eq:amplification-B0}; transpose symmetry, and nothing
else, supplies the projection.  \Cref{rem:parity} identifies that hypothesis as
a $\Z/2$-degree condition and shows what the sharpening is worth: the
coefficient $1/r$ of $\abs{\Tr C}^2$ in \cref{thm:rank_r} rather than $1$.

In the transpose-symmetric case \cref{eq:amplification-B} is
\cref{thm:pair-amplification}: it says that
\[
    \Psi_{r,\Phi}
    :=
    \Phi\circ\tau
    +(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\left(\Delta-\tfrac1r\id\right)
\]
is \emph{$r$-positive}, meaning that
$(\Psi_{r,\Phi}\otimes\id_{\C^r})(\rho)\succeq0$ for every positive
semidefinite $\rho$ on $(U\otimes V)\otimes\C^r$ --- equivalently, by the
Choi--Jamiołkowski correspondence used throughout \cref{sec:applications}, that
its Choi operator has nonnegative expectation on every vector of Schmidt rank at
most $r$.  \Cref{rem:operator-ineq-as-map} carries out that translation and
identifies the resulting map.

The proof occupies \cref{sec:proof}; \cref{eq:amplification-A} is
\cref{eq:Phi-Pminus-goal} with $\beta_2$ in place of $1$, and each of
\cref{eq:amplification-B0,eq:amplification-B} follows from the display above it
because $P_+$ survives $\Phi$ and $P_+-P_-$ is twice the partial transpose.  The
constant $\beta_2$ enters through one estimate on the Kraus span
(\cref{eq:kraus-pair-bound}), and transpose symmetry through one orthogonality
(\cref{eq:T-orthogonal}).  Nothing else about $\Phi$ is used --- in particular
nothing about $\mathfrak{so}(U)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(V)$ beyond
\cref{eq:beta2-double-skew}.

At $\Phi=\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$ and $\beta_2=1$, \cref{thm:amplification} is
the operator inequality we now record in the form \cref{sec:proof} uses.

\begin{proposition}[Rank-$r$ marginal operator inequality]
\label[proposition]{prop:operator_ineq}
Let $Q=\C^r$, let $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ be an orthonormal family in
$U\otimes V$, and let $q_1,\ldots,q_r$ be an orthonormal basis of
$Q$. Define the unnormalized code vector and its rank-one operator by
\[
    \delta_e:=\sum_{i=1}^r e_i\otimes q_i,
    \qquad
    \rho_0:=\ketbra{\delta_e}{\delta_e},
    \qquad
    \Pi_e:=\frac1r\rho_0.
\]
Thus $\Pi_e$ is the orthogonal projection onto
$\operatorname{span}\{\delta_e\}$.  Write
\[
    \rho^0_{UQ}:=\Tr_V\rho_0,
    \qquad
    \rho^0_{VQ}:=\Tr_U\rho_0.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H_r}
    H_r^{(0)}
    :=
    rI_{UVQ}+\Pi_e
    -\iota_{UQ}(\rho^0_{UQ})
    -\iota_{VQ}(\rho^0_{VQ})
    \succeq0.
\end{equation}
For $S\subseteq\{U,V,Q\}$, the map
\[
    \iota_S:
    \cL\!\left(\bigotimes_{X\in S}X\right)
    \longrightarrow \cL(U\otimes V\otimes Q)
\]
uses the order inherited from $U,V,Q$: it tensors with identities on
omitted factors and conjugates by the unique permutation unitary that
restores the order $U\otimes V\otimes Q$.  For example,
$\iota_{UQ}(X)$ is the operator that acts as $X$ on the labeled
$U,Q$ factors and as the identity on $V$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix orthonormal bases of $U$ and $V$, relative to which transposition
and the skew-symmetric operator spaces are defined. Although the proof
uses these bases, the resulting operator inequality is basis
independent.

Expanding $\rho_0$ and transposing the $UV$ factor gives
\begin{align}
    \rho_0^{T_{UV}}
    &=
    \sum_{i,j=1}^r
    \bigl(\ketbra{e_i}{e_j}\bigr)^T
    \otimes\ketbra{q_i}{q_j}
    \notag\\
    &=
    \sum_{i,j=1}^r
    \ketbra{\overline e_j\otimes q_i}
            {\overline e_i\otimes q_j}.
\label{eq:rho-partial-transpose-expanded}
\end{align}
Let
\[
    S:=\operatorname{span}
    \{\overline e_1,\ldots,\overline e_r\},
\]
and let $J:Q\to S$ be the unitary determined by
$Jq_i=\overline e_i$. Define a self-adjoint unitary $F_J$ on
$S\otimes Q$ by
\[
    F_J(s\otimes q)
    :=Jq\otimes J^{-1}s.
\]
In particular,
\[
    F_J(\overline e_i\otimes q_j)
    =\overline e_j\otimes q_i.
\]
Thus the sum in \cref{eq:rho-partial-transpose-expanded} is $F_J$ on
$S\otimes Q$. Let
\[
    P_\pm:=\frac12(I_{S\otimes Q}\pm F_J)
\]
be its positive and negative eigenspace projections, extended by zero
on $(S\otimes Q)^\perp$; thus $P_++P_-$ is the orthogonal projection
onto $S\otimes Q$, not the identity on $U\otimes V\otimes Q$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rho-partial-transpose}
    \rho_0^{T_{UV}}
    =P_+-P_-.
\end{equation}
Explicitly,
\[
    P_-
    =
    \sum_{1\le i<j\le r}
    \ketbra{\eta_{ij}}{\eta_{ij}},
\]
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-ij}
    \eta_{ij}
    =
    \frac1{\sqrt2}
    \bigl(
      \overline e_i\otimes q_j
      -
      \overline e_j\otimes q_i
    \bigr).
\end{equation}

For a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $E$, define
\[
    \Lambda_E(X)
    :=
    \Tr(X)I_E-X^T.
\]
If $L_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Lambda-kraus}
    \Lambda_E(X)
    =
    \sum_{a<b}L_{ab}XL_{ab}^*.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Lambda_E$ is completely positive. Equivalently, its Choi
operator is
\[
    J(\Lambda_E)
    =
    \sum_{i,j}
    \ketbra{i}{j}\otimes\Lambda_E(E_{ij})
    =I-F,
\]
whose spectrum is contained in $\{0,2\}$.

Define
\[
    \Phi
    :=
    \Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V\otimes\id_Q.
\]
For an operator $Y$ on $U\otimes V\otimes Q$, direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Phi-expansion}
\begin{aligned}
    \Phi(Y)
    ={}&
    \iota_Q(\Tr_{UV}Y)
    -\iota_{VQ}\!\left((\Tr_UY)^{T_V}\right)\\
    &-\iota_{UQ}\!\left((\Tr_VY)^{T_U}\right)
    +Y^{T_{UV}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Applying \cref{eq:Phi-expansion} to $Y=\rho_0^{T_{UV}}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Phi-rhoT}
    \Phi(\rho_0^{T_{UV}})
    =
    I_{UVQ}
    -\iota_{UQ}(\rho^0_{UQ})
    -\iota_{VQ}(\rho^0_{VQ})
    +\rho_0.
\end{equation}
Using \cref{eq:rho-partial-transpose}, we therefore obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H-decomposition}
    H_r^{(0)}
    =
    \Phi(P_+)
    +
    \Bigl[
      (r-1)(I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e)-\Phi(P_-)
    \Bigr].
\end{equation}
Since $\Phi(P_+)\succeq0$, it remains to prove
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Phi-Pminus-goal}
    \Phi(P_-)
    \preceq
    (r-1)(I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e).
\end{equation}

Let $\{L_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_U}$ and
$\{M_\beta\}_{\beta\in\mathcal A_V}$ be the standard
skew-symmetric Kraus operators for $\Lambda_U$ and $\Lambda_V$,
normalized by
\[
    \langle L_\alpha,L_{\alpha'}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
    =2\delta_{\alpha\alpha'},
    \qquad
    \langle M_\beta,M_{\beta'}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
    =2\delta_{\beta\beta'}.
\]
Put
\[
    \mathcal I_r
    :=
    \{(i,j,\alpha,\beta):
       1\le i<j\le r,
       \ \alpha\in\mathcal A_U,
       \ \beta\in\mathcal A_V\}.
\]
Define
$\mathcal T:\ell_2(\mathcal I_r)\to U\otimes V\otimes Q$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:T-definition}
    \mathcal Tc
    :=
    \sum_{1\le i<j\le r}
    \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
    c_{\alpha\beta}^{(ij)}
    (L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta\otimes I_Q)\eta_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Thus the column indexed by
$(i,j,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal I_r$ is
$(L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta\otimes I_Q)\eta_{ij}$.  Using the Kraus
form of $\Phi$ and the rank-one expansion of $P_-$ therefore gives
\begin{align}
    \Phi(P_-)
    &=
    \sum_{i<j}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
    \ketbra{
      (L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta\otimes I_Q)\eta_{ij}}
      {(L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta\otimes I_Q)\eta_{ij}}
    \notag\\
    &=\mathcal T\mathcal T^*.
\label{eq:Phi-Pminus-TTstar}
\end{align}

For each edge $(i,j)$ of the complete graph $K_r$, define
\[
    K_c^{(ij)}
    :=
    \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
    c_{\alpha\beta}^{(ij)}
    L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta.
\]
Then $K_c^{(ij)}$ lies in the linear span of
\[
    \{L\otimes M:
      L\in\mathfrak{so}(U),\ M\in\mathfrak{so}(V)\},
\]
and orthogonality of the Kraus operators gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Kc-norm}
\begin{aligned}
    \norm{K_c^{(ij)}}_2^2
    &=
    \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
    \abs{c_{\alpha\beta}^{(ij)}}^2
    \norm{L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta}_2^2\\
    &=4\norm{c^{(ij)}}_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Grouping the output of $\mathcal T$ according to the basis
$\{q_k\}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:T-by-vertices}
    \mathcal Tc
    =
    \frac1{\sqrt2}
    \sum_{k=1}^r
    \left(
      \sum_{i<k}K_c^{(ik)}\overline e_i
      -
      \sum_{k<j}K_c^{(kj)}\overline e_j
    \right)
    \otimes q_k.
\end{equation}
At each vertex $k$, there are $r-1$ incident terms. Hence
Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\begin{align}
    \norm{\mathcal Tc}^2
    &\le
    \frac{r-1}{2}
    \sum_{k=1}^r
    \left(
      \sum_{i<k}
      \norm{K_c^{(ik)}\overline e_i}^2
      +
      \sum_{k<j}
      \norm{K_c^{(kj)}\overline e_j}^2
    \right)
    \notag\\
    &=
    \frac{r-1}{2}
    \sum_{1\le i<j\le r}
    \left(
      \norm{K_c^{(ij)}\overline e_i}^2
      +
      \norm{K_c^{(ij)}\overline e_j}^2
    \right).
\label{eq:T-Cauchy}
\end{align}
Each parenthesized expression is bounded by $\beta_2(\Phi)$ directly, without
\cref{eq:Kc-norm} and without any property of
$\mathfrak{so}(U)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(V)$.  Write $\{A_a\}$ for the Kraus family
of $\Phi$ on $\cL(U\otimes V)$, here $A_{\alpha\beta}=L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta$,
and let $S:c\mapsto\sum_ac_aA_a$ be its synthesis operator, so that
$SS^*=J(\Phi)$ under $\operatorname{vec}$.  For an orthonormal pair $x,y$ and
$P:=\ketbra xx+\ketbra yy$, self-adjoint idempotence of $P$ gives
$\norm{KP}_2=\sup\{\abs{\ip MK_{\mathrm{HS}}}:\rank M\le2,\ \norm M_2=1\}$,
so with $K=Sc$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kraus-cauchy-schwarz}
    \norm{Kx}^2+\norm{Ky}^2
    =\norm{KP}_2^2
    =\sup_M\abs{\ip{S^*M}{c}}^2
    \le\norm c_2^2\,\sup_M\norm{S^*M}_2^2
    =\norm c_2^2\,\norm{J(\Phi)}_{S(2)},
\end{equation}
the suprema being over $\rank M\le2$, $\norm M_2=1$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kraus-pair-bound}
    \norm{Kx}^2+\norm{Ky}^2
    \le
    2\beta_2(\Phi)\norm c_2^2 .
\end{equation}
This is $\norm S^2\le\norm{SS^*}$ in the $S(2)$-norm, and is the elementary
half of the projection duality of \cite{JohnstonKribs2010}; that a rank-two
compression suffices to test the pair is
\cite[Prop.~1.1]{TakasakiTomiyama1983}.  Neither minimality nor orthogonality of
$\{A_a\}$ is used --- the projection $Q_-$ enters only through the number
$\beta_2(\Phi)$, and $\Pi K=K$ is replaced by membership of $K$ in the Kraus
span.  Substituting \cref{eq:kraus-pair-bound} into \cref{eq:T-Cauchy},
\begin{align}
    \norm{\mathcal Tc}^2
    &\le
    \frac{r-1}{2}\cdot2\beta_2(\Phi)
    \sum_{i<j}\norm{c^{(ij)}}_2^2
    \notag\\
    &=(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\norm{c}_2^2 .
\label{eq:T-operator-bound}
\end{align}
For $\Phi=\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$ this is $(r-1)\norm c_2^2$ by
\cref{eq:beta2-double-skew}, and the estimate is attained already when
$r=2$ and $\dim U=\dim V=2$.  Then every vertex has degree one and
$\mathfrak{so}(2)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(2)$ is one-dimensional; its
nonzero elements are scalar multiples of a unitary, so every
orthonormal pair saturates \cref{eq:double-skew-action}.  There is no
slack anywhere in that $r=2$ chain.

Thus
\[
    \mathcal T\mathcal T^*
    \preceq
    (r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\,I_{UVQ}.
\]
It remains to use the orthogonality of the range of $\mathcal T$ to
$\delta_e$. Since each tensor product of two skew-symmetric operators
is symmetric,
\[
    K^T=K
    \qquad
    \text{for every }K
    \text{ in the above linear span}.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align}
    \ip{\delta_e}{\mathcal Tc}
    &=
    \frac1{\sqrt2}
    \sum_{i<j}
    \left(
      \overline e_j^T
      K_c^{(ij)}
      \overline e_i
      -
      \overline e_i^T
      K_c^{(ij)}
      \overline e_j
    \right)
    \notag\\
    &=0.
\label{eq:T-orthogonal}
\end{align}
Hence
\[
    \ran\mathcal T\subseteq\delta_e^\perp,
    \qquad
    P_{\delta_e^\perp}=I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e.
\]
Since \cref{eq:T-operator-bound} says that
$\norm{\mathcal T}^2\le(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)$, for every vector $x$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \ip{x}{\mathcal T\mathcal T^*x}
    &=
    \ip{P_{\delta_e^\perp}x}
       {\mathcal T\mathcal T^*P_{\delta_e^\perp}x}\\
    &\le
    (r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\norm{P_{\delta_e^\perp}x}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
    \mathcal T\mathcal T^*
    \preceq
    (r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)(I_{UVQ}-\Pi_e).
\]
This is \cref{eq:amplification-A}, and \cref{eq:beta2-double-skew} specializes
it to \cref{eq:Phi-Pminus-goal}, whence $H_r^{(0)}\succeq0$ by
\cref{eq:H-decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:amplification}]
The argument just given uses $\Phi$ only through three facts, and they enter at
separate points.  That $\Phi$ is a Kraus sum gives $\Phi(P_+)\succeq0$ and
\cref{eq:Phi-Pminus-TTstar}.  That its Kraus span obeys
\cref{eq:kraus-pair-bound} --- which needs only $\beta_2(\Phi)$ --- gives
\cref{eq:T-operator-bound}, hence
$\mathcal T\mathcal T^*\preceq(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)I$, which is
\cref{eq:amplification-A0}.  Transpose symmetry of the Kraus operators enters
only in \cref{eq:T-orthogonal}, and only to replace $I$ by $I-\Pi_e$, giving
\cref{eq:amplification-A}.

Each of \cref{eq:amplification-B0,eq:amplification-B} follows from the
corresponding upper bound: $\rho_0^{T_{UV}}=P_+-P_-$ and $\Phi(P_+)\succeq0$,
so $\Phi(\rho_0^{T_{UV}})\succeq-\Phi(P_-)$.

For the $r$-positivity assertion no further identity is needed.  Since the $e_i$
are orthonormal, $\Tr_{UV}\rho_0=I_Q$ and $\rho_0=r\Pi_e$, so
\[
    I-\Pi_e
    =\bigl[(\Delta-\tfrac1r\id)\otimes\id_Q\bigr](\rho_0),
    \qquad
    (\Phi\circ\tau\otimes\id_Q)(\rho_0)
    =(\Phi\otimes\id_Q)\bigl(\rho_0^{T_{UV}}\bigr),
\]
so \cref{eq:amplification-B} is exactly
$(\Psi_{r,\Phi}\otimes\id_Q)(\rho_0)\succeq0$.  A vector of Schmidt rank at most
$r$ is $(I\otimes D)\delta_e$ for some $D$ and some orthonormal
$e_1,\ldots,e_r$, by \cref{eq:C-decomposition}; conjugating the previous display
by $I\otimes D$ preserves positivity and evaluates it there.  Both steps use
only that $\Phi$ is completely positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Transpose parity, and where the trace term comes from]
\label[remark]{rem:parity}
Since $\operatorname{vec}(K^T)=F\operatorname{vec}(K)$ for the flip $F$ on the
two vectorization slots, transposition is an involution of the Kraus space and
grades it by the flip eigenvalue: $\wedge^2\!H$ has degree $1$ and
$\operatorname{Sym}^2\!H$ degree $0$.  Kraus spaces multiply under $\otimes$ and
the degree is additive, so the grading is by $\Z/2$.  In this language:

\begin{quote}
The hypothesis of \cref{eq:T-orthogonal} is that the Kraus space has degree
$0$; each $\mathfrak{so}$ factor has degree $1$; hence exactly the products of
an \emph{even} number of skew factors qualify.
\end{quote}

Concretely, every $K$ in the span of $\{L\otimes M\}$ satisfies $K^T=K$, which
is what makes the two terms of \cref{eq:T-orthogonal} cancel.  In odd degree
$K^T=-K$, the two terms add, and $\ran\mathcal T$ does not lie in
$\delta_e^\perp$; the argument then yields only
$\mathcal T\mathcal T^*\preceq(r-1)I$ in place of $(r-1)(I-\Pi_e)$, giving
$rI+\rho_0-\iota_{UQ}(\rho^0_{UQ})-\iota_{VQ}(\rho^0_{VQ})\succeq0$ and hence
the coefficient $1$ rather than the sharp $1/r$ in front of $\abs{\Tr C}^2$.

So the $\frac1r\abs{\Tr C}^2$ term of \cref{thm:rank_r} is exactly the
contribution of the single protected direction $\delta_e$, and the grading is
what protects it: the generalizations that keep the sharp trace coefficient are
the even-degree ones.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The marginal inequality as an $r$-positivity statement]
\label[remark]{rem:operator-ineq-as-map}
Recall $\Delta$ and $\tau$ from \cref{thm:amplification}, and
$\mathcal R_\alpha(X)=\Tr(X)I+\alpha X$ from \cref{cor:tensor-square}.  Put
\[
    S:=\mathcal R_{-1}=\Delta-\id,
    \qquad
    t:=\frac{r-1}{r},
    \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
    \mathcal R_{-1/r}=S+t\,\id .
\]

Two facts suffice.  First, both $\Delta$ and $\tau$ factor across tensor
products: $\tau_{U\otimes V}=\tau_U\otimes\tau_V$, and
$\Delta_{U\otimes V}=\Delta_U\otimes\Delta_V$ because discarding a composite
system and repreparing it is discarding and repreparing each factor.  Since
$\Lambda(Y)=\Tr(Y)I-Y^T$ gives $\Lambda\circ\tau=\Delta-\id=S$ on a single
factor,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lambda-tau-monoidal}
    (\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V)\circ\tau
    =(\Lambda_U\circ\tau_U)\otimes(\Lambda_V\circ\tau_V)
    =S_U\otimes S_V .
\end{equation}
Second, $\{\id,\Delta\}$ spans a two-dimensional commutative algebra, and the
rest is a binomial expansion in it: using $\Delta=S+\id$ on each factor,
\begin{align*}
    S\otimes S+(r-1)\,\Delta_U\otimes\Delta_V-t\,\id
    &=S\otimes S+(r-1)(S+\id)\otimes(S+\id)-t\,\id\\
    &=r\,S\otimes S+(r-1)(S\otimes\id+\id\otimes S)+\tfrac{(r-1)^2}{r}\,\id\\
    &=r\bigl[S\otimes S+t(S\otimes\id+\id\otimes S)+t^2\,\id\bigr],
\end{align*}
by $rt=r-1$ and $rt^2=(r-1)^2/r$.  The bracket is
$(S+t\,\id)^{\otimes2}$, so for every $r\ge1$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:map-identity}
    (\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V)\circ\tau
    +(r-1)\left(\Delta-\tfrac1r\id\right)
    =
    r\,\mathcal R^U_{-1/r}\otimes\mathcal R^V_{-1/r}.
\end{equation}

Now $\Tr_{UV}\rho_0=I_Q$ because the $e_i$ are orthonormal, and
$\rho_0=r\Pi_e$, so
$I-\Pi_e=\bigl[(\Delta-\tfrac1r\id)\otimes\id_Q\bigr](\rho_0)$.  Combining
this with \cref{eq:Phi-rhoT} and \cref{eq:map-identity} rewrites \cref{eq:H_r}
as
\[
    H_r^{(0)}
    =
    \bigl[
      \bigl(r\,\mathcal R^U_{-1/r}\otimes\mathcal R^V_{-1/r}\bigr)
      \otimes\id_Q
    \bigr](\rho_0).
\]
Thus \cref{prop:operator_ineq} states precisely that
$\mathcal R_{-1/r}\otimes\mathcal R_{-1/r}$ is $r$-positive.  The passage from
orthonormal frames $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ to arbitrary vectors of Schmidt rank at most
$r$ is the range factorization \cref{eq:C-decomposition}, which produces the
frame $\{e_i\}$ and the test vector $\delta_d$ in one step; no filtering or
limiting argument is involved.  Equivalently, this is the sufficiency half of
\cref{cor:asymmetric-block-positive} at $a=b=1/r$, available here, before
\cref{thm:rank_r}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the correction term has this shape]
\label[remark]{rem:covariance}
$\cL(H)=\C I\oplus\{\text{traceless}\}$ is a decomposition into inequivalent
$\mathrm U(d)$-representations, so the commutant --- the $\mathrm
U(d)$-covariant superoperators on $\cL(H)$ --- is exactly
$\operatorname{span}\{\id,\Delta\}$, of dimension two, with $\mathcal R_{-a}$
acting as $d-a$ on $\C I$ and as $-a$ on the traceless part.  A covariant
correction to $\Phi\circ\tau$ therefore lies in a one-parameter pencil once its
scale is fixed, and $1/r$ is the single free coefficient; \cref{rem:parity}
identifies what pins it, namely the protected direction $\delta_e$.  The
correction term is not a choice.  The geometry of that pencil --- the segment
from $\id$ to $\Delta$, its extension, and the nested $k$-positive cones along
it --- is the subject of \cite{Tomiyama1985}, and $\mathcal R_{-1/r}$ is its
$r$-positivity boundary.

The collapse in \cref{eq:map-identity} is specific to two factors.  For $n$
factors the same factorization gives
$\Delta=\sum_{T\subseteq[n]}\bigotimes_{i\in T}S_i$, and matching against
$c\bigotimes(S+t\,\id)$ forces $r-1=r\,t^{\,n-\abs T}$ for every $T$ with
$\abs T<n$.  At $\abs T=n-1$ this reads $r-1=rt$, which holds; for $n=2$ the
$-t\,\id$ term repairs $\abs T=0$; but for $n\ge3$ the equation at
$\abs T=n-2$ is $r-1=(r-1)^2/r$, i.e.\ $r=1$.  So the even-partite
generalizations of \cref{rem:parity} are genuine inequalities and cannot be
recast as a map identity of the form $\bigotimes\mathcal R_{-a}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Proof of the Rank-\texorpdfstring{$r$}{r}
Partial-Trace Inequality}
\label{sec:proof}

\begin{lemma}[Partial-trace contraction identities]
\label[lemma]{lem:partial-trace-contractions}
Let $e_1,\ldots,e_s\in U\otimes V$ be orthonormal, let
$d_1,\ldots,d_s\in U\otimes V$, and let $q_1,\ldots,q_s$ be an
orthonormal basis of $Q=\C^s$. Define
\[
    C:=\sum_{i=1}^s\ketbra{e_i}{d_i},
    \qquad
    \delta_e:=\sum_{i=1}^s e_i\otimes q_i,
    \qquad
    \rho_0:=\ketbra{\delta_e}{\delta_e},
\]
and
\[
    \delta_d:=\sum_{i=1}^s d_i\otimes q_i,
    \qquad
    \rho^0_{UQ}:=\Tr_V\rho_0,
    \qquad
    \rho^0_{VQ}:=\Tr_U\rho_0.
\]
Then
\begin{align}
    \ip{\delta_d}{\delta_d}
    &=\norm{C}_2^2,
\label{eq:contraction-norm}\\
    \ip{\delta_d}{\rho_0\delta_d}
    &=\abs{\Tr C}^2,
\label{eq:contraction-trace}\\
    \ip{\delta_d}{\iota_{UQ}(\rho^0_{UQ})\delta_d}
    &=\norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2,
\label{eq:contraction-U}\\
    \ip{\delta_d}{\iota_{VQ}(\rho^0_{VQ})\delta_d}
    &=\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2.
\label{eq:contraction-V}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first identity follows from the orthonormality of the $e_i$:
\[
    \norm{C}_2^2
    =\sum_i\norm{d_i}^2
    =\ip{\delta_d}{\delta_d}.
\]
Since $\Tr C=\sum_i\ip{d_i}{e_i}$ under the stated inner-product
convention,
\[
    \ip{\delta_e}{\delta_d}
    =\sum_i\ip{e_i}{d_i}
    =\overline{\Tr C},
\]
which proves \cref{eq:contraction-trace}.

For the first marginal contraction,
\[
    \rho^0_{UQ}
    =\sum_{i,j}
      \Tr_V\ketbra{e_i}{e_j}\otimes\ketbra{q_i}{q_j},
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contraction-U-expansion}
    \ip{\delta_d}{\iota_{UQ}(\rho^0_{UQ})\delta_d}
    =\sum_{i,j}
      \ip{d_i}{
        (\Tr_V\ketbra{e_i}{e_j}\otimes I_V)d_j
      }.
\end{equation}
Choose product bases $\{u_a\}$ of $U$ and $\{v_b\}$ of $V$, and
write
\[
    e_i=\sum_{a,b}e_{i,ab}u_a\otimes v_b,
    \qquad
    d_i=\sum_{a,b}d_{i,ab}u_a\otimes v_b.
\]
Then
\[
    (\Tr_UC)_{b,c}
    =\sum_{i,a}e_{i,ab}\overline{d_{i,ac}},
\]
so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contraction-U-common-sum}
    \norm{\Tr_UC}_2^2
    =\sum_{i,j}\sum_{a,a'}\sum_{b,c}
      e_{i,ab}\overline{d_{i,ac}}
      \overline{e_{j,a'b}}d_{j,a'c}.
\end{equation}
Expanding the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:contraction-U-expansion} gives, explicitly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    &\sum_{i,j}
      \ip{d_i}{
        (\Tr_V\ketbra{e_i}{e_j}\otimes I_V)d_j
      }\\
    &\qquad=
    \sum_{i,j}\sum_{a,a'}\sum_{b,c}
      e_{i,ab}\overline{d_{i,ac}}
      \overline{e_{j,a'b}}d_{j,a'c},
\end{aligned}
\]
the same quadruple sum as in
\cref{eq:contraction-U-common-sum}.  This proves
\cref{eq:contraction-U}. Interchanging $U$ and $V$ proves
\cref{eq:contraction-V}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:rank_r}]
The case $C=0$ is immediate. Suppose $C\ne0$, and let
\[
    r_0:=\rank C.
\]
Choose an orthonormal basis
$\{e_i\}_{i=1}^{r_0}$ of $\operatorname{ran}C$ and set
\[
    d_i:=C^*e_i.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:C-decomposition}
    C
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\ketbra{e_i}{d_i},
    \qquad
    \sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\norm{d_i}^2
    =\norm{C}_2^2.
\end{equation}
Since $\rank C\le r$, one has $1\le r_0\le r$.

Let $Q=\C^{r_0}$, with orthonormal basis
$\{q_i\}_{i=1}^{r_0}$, and define
\[
    \delta_e:=\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}e_i\otimes q_i,
    \qquad
    \rho_0:=\ketbra{\delta_e}{\delta_e},
    \qquad
    \Pi_e:=\frac1{r_0}\rho_0,
    \qquad
    \delta_d:=\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}d_i\otimes q_i.
\]
Write $\rho^0_{UQ}=\Tr_V\rho_0$ and
$\rho^0_{VQ}=\Tr_U\rho_0$. Applying
\cref{prop:operator_ineq} with $r=r_0$ gives
$H_{r_0}^{(0)}\succeq0$. Therefore,
\begin{align}
    0
    &\le
    \ip{\delta_d}{H_{r_0}^{(0)}\delta_d}
    \notag\\
    &=
    r_0\ip{\delta_d}{\delta_d}
    +\frac1{r_0}\ip{\delta_d}{\rho_0\delta_d}
    -\ip{\delta_d}{\iota_{UQ}(\rho^0_{UQ})\delta_d}
    -\ip{\delta_d}{\iota_{VQ}(\rho^0_{VQ})\delta_d}.
\label{eq:H-expectation}
\end{align}
By \cref{lem:partial-trace-contractions}, this is precisely
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:main-bound-r0}
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2
    \le
    r_0\norm{C}_2^2
    +\frac1{r_0}\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\end{equation}

It remains to pass from $r_0$ to $r$.  From
\cref{eq:C-decomposition} and
$\Tr C=\sum_i\ip{d_i}{e_i}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \abs{\Tr C}^2
    &\le
    \left(\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\norm{d_i}\right)^2\\
    &\le
    r_0\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\norm{d_i}^2
    =r_0\norm{C}_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The passage from $r_0$ to $r$ is then a single identity: for all
$r\ge r_0\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rank-monotonicity}
    r\norm{C}_2^2+\frac1r\abs{\Tr C}^2
    -\left(
      r_0\norm{C}_2^2+\frac1{r_0}\abs{\Tr C}^2
    \right)
    =
    (r-r_0)
    \left(
      \norm{C}_2^2-\frac{\abs{\Tr C}^2}{rr_0}
    \right).
\end{equation}
By the trace--rank bound the second factor is at least
$\bigl(1-\frac1r\bigr)\norm{C}_2^2\ge0$, and the first is nonnegative, so the
right-hand side is nonnegative.  Combining this with \cref{eq:main-bound-r0}
proves \cref{eq:main-bound}.

Both factors are moreover \emph{strictly} positive when $C\ne0$ and $r_0<r$:
the first is at least $1$, and $r_0<r$ forces $r\ge2$, so
$1-\frac1r\ge\frac12$ while $\norm{C}_2^2>0$.  Hence the inequality of
\cref{eq:main-bound} is strict whenever $\rank C<r$ and $C\ne0$, which is the
exact-rank assertion of \cref{rem:optimality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What $\beta_2$ does and does not carry]
\label[remark]{rem:constant-dependence}
\Cref{thm:amplification} depends on $\Phi$ only through $\beta_2(\Phi)$.  The
passage from it to a \emph{partial-trace} inequality does not: the marginals
$\Tr_UC$ and $\Tr_VC$ appear only through \cref{eq:Phi-rhoT}, a contraction
identity for the particular map $\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$, and no such
identity is available for a general $\Phi$.  A second map with the same
$\beta_2$ contracts against $\delta_d$ to a different quadratic form.

That $\beta_2$ alone cannot suffice is visible at $\Phi=0$, which satisfies
every hypothesis of \cref{thm:amplification} with $\beta_2=0$: the extremizer
$C=P_r\otimes\ketbra vw$ of \cref{rem:optimality} with $v\perp w$ has
$\norm{\Tr_UC}_2^2=r^2$, $\Tr_VC=0$, $\norm C_2^2=r$ and $\Tr C=0$, so no
inequality with $\Phi$-independent coefficients degenerating at $\beta_2=0$ can
hold.  What survives at general $\beta_2$ is the operator statement
\cref{eq:amplification-A,eq:amplification-B} and, for
$\Phi=\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$ with the constant left free, the family
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:main-bound-parametrized}
    \norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2
    \le
    \bigl[r+(\beta_2-1)(r-1)\bigr]\norm{C}_2^2
    +\bigl[1-(\beta_2-1)(r-1)\bigr]\frac1r\abs{\Tr C}^2
\end{equation}
for nonzero $C$ with $r=\rank C$, obtained by carrying $\beta_2$ rather than $1$
through \cref{eq:T-operator-bound}.  At $\beta_2=1$ this is
\cref{eq:main-bound-exact-rank}.

The coefficient $(r-1)\beta_2$ in \cref{thm:amplification} bounds the correction
rather than computing it: the vertex step \cref{eq:T-Cauchy} discards the
nonnegative variance terms of \cref{prop:sos-complete-graph}, so for a general
$\Phi$ a smaller multiple of $\Delta-\frac1r\id$ may already suffice.  For
$\Phi=\Lambda_U\otimes\Lambda_V$ the value $\beta_2=1$ cannot be improved: as
noted after \cref{eq:T-operator-bound} it is already attained at
$\dim U=\dim V=2$, where $\mathfrak{so}(2)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(2)$ is
one-dimensional and every orthonormal pair saturates
\cref{eq:double-skew-action}.  The extremizer of \cref{rem:optimality} forces
$\beta_2\ge1$ there unconditionally, so \cref{eq:main-bound-parametrized} is
never stronger than \cref{thm:rank_r} on that space.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Two limits of the method]
\label[remark]{rem:scope}
\Cref{thm:amplification} does not extend in either of the two directions one
would try first.

\emph{Schmidt rank $p$.}  \Cref{eq:kraus-cauchy-schwarz} generalizes at once: a
rank-$p$ compression $P$ and the $S(p)$-norm of $J(\Phi)$ give
$\sum_{i\le p}\norm{Kx_i}^2\le p\,\beta_p(\Phi)\norm c_2^2$ for any orthonormal
$p$-tuple.  The reduction does not.  The pair structure of
\cref{sec:proof} comes from the partial transpose and not from the Schmidt
rank: $P_\pm$ are the $\pm1$ eigenspaces of the flip that
$\rho_0^{T_{UV}}$ decomposes into, and $\eta_{ij}$, $\zeta_{ij}$, and the edge
set of $K_r$ are pair-indexed downstream of that.  A $p$-fold analogue would
have to decompose the symmetric group $S_p$, which is a different problem.  We
record it as open.

\emph{Higher copies.}  By \cref{rem:parity}, $\Phi=\Lambda_{U_1}\otimes\cdots
\otimes\Lambda_{U_{2m}}$ has degree $0$ and so satisfies the hypothesis of
\cref{thm:amplification} for every $m$; expanding $\Phi\circ\tau$ then gives,
for $\rank C\le r$,
\[
    \sum_{S\subseteq[2m]}(-1)^{\abs S}\norm{\Tr_SC}_2^2
    +(r-1)\beta_2(\Phi)\left(\norm C_2^2-\tfrac1r\abs{\Tr C}^2\right)
    \ge0 .
\]
At $m=1$ this is \cref{eq:main-bound-exact-rank}.  What is missing is the
constant: $\beta_2(\Lambda^{\otimes2m})$ for $m\ge2$ is a Ky-Fan-$2$
concentration constant for the $2m$-fold antisymmetric projection, and we do not
know its value.  So the family \emph{reduces} to one rank-two Choi $S(2)$-norm
per $m$; it does not follow from \cref{lem:double-skew}.  By
\cref{rem:covariance} it also cannot be recast as a map identity.
\end{remark}

\section{Applications and Corollaries}
\label{sec:applications}

This section collects consequences of the results proved above: the exact
symmetric score curve, an asymmetric tensor-product score theorem, block
positivity and positive maps, quantitative Schmidt-number separation, Ky
Fan bounds for Kronecker sums, and the exact Schmidt number of a product
of antisymmetric projector states.  None of them is part of the
machine-checked development.

They do not all rest on the same input.
\Cref{prop:exact-q2-score,thm:exact-asymmetric-score,cor:kronecker} use
\cref{thm:rank_r} at general $r$, and are the results for which the
extension beyond normal matrices is what is needed;
\cref{cor:two-copy-block-positive,cor:tensor-square,cor:asymmetric-block-positive}
restate them through the Choi--Jamiołkowski correspondence.
\Cref{cor:exact-schmidt-number,cor:explicit-witnesses}, by contrast, rest
on \cref{lem:double-skew} alone and do not use \cref{thm:rank_r}.  \Cref{app:generic} collects
two further consequences that use nothing about the witnesses constructed
here beyond $r$-block positivity.

\subsection{Two-copy block positivity}

Fix $d\ge2$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb R$. Let
\[
    \rho_\alpha
    :=
    I+\alpha F
\]
be the unnormalized Werner operator on $\C^d\otimes\C^d$; for
$\alpha\in[-1,1]$, it is proportional to the standard Werner state
\cite{Werner1989}. Its partial transpose is
\[
    \rho_\alpha^\Gamma
    =
    I+\alpha\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega},
    \qquad
    \Omega
    :=
    \sum_{i=1}^d e_i\otimes e_i.
\]
The case $\alpha\ge0$ is immediate because
\[
    I+\alpha\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}\succeq0.
\]

For $\alpha\le0$ and $C\in M_{d^2}(\C)$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:q2}
    q^{(2)}(\alpha,C)
    :=
    \norm{C}_2^2
    +
    \alpha\left(
      \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2
    \right)
    +
    \alpha^2\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\end{equation}
The first argument is the Werner parameter of $\rho_\alpha$; only
$\alpha\le0$ is at issue, and it is convenient to write $\alpha=-t$ with
$t\ge0$, so that
\[
    q^{(2)}(-t,C)
    =
    \norm{C}_2^2
    -
    t\left(
      \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2+\norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2
    \right)
    +
    t^2\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\]

An operator is called $r$-block-positive if its expectation is
nonnegative on every vector of Schmidt rank at most $r$. With the
vectorization convention fixed in the introduction and the regrouping
$(A_1A_2):(B_1B_2)$, one has
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:q2-vectorization}
\begin{aligned}
    &\left\langle
      \operatorname{vec}(C),
      \bigl(\rho_\alpha^\Gamma\bigr)^{\otimes2}
      \operatorname{vec}(C)
    \right\rangle\\
    &\qquad =q^{(2)}(\alpha,C),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which is what the first argument of $q^{(2)}$ records.
Moreover,
\[
    \SR\bigl(\operatorname{vec}(C)\bigr)=\rank C
\]
across the same output--input regrouping. Thus $r$-block positivity of
$(\rho_{-t}^\Gamma)^{\otimes2}$ is equivalent to $q^{(2)}(-t,C)\ge0$ for
every matrix $C$ of rank at most $r$.
For every $C$ of rank at most $r$, \cref{eq:main-bound} implies
\begin{align}
    q^{(2)}(-t,C)
    &\ge
    (1-rt)\norm{C}_2^2
    +
    \left(
      t^2-\frac tr
    \right)\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\label{eq:q2-first-bound}
\end{align}
If $0\le t\le1/r$, then
$t^2-t/r\le0$. Using
\[
    \abs{\Tr C}^2
    \le
    r\norm{C}_2^2
\]
in the appropriate direction gives
\begin{align}
    q^{(2)}(-t,C)
    &\ge
    \left(
      1-rt+rt^2-t
    \right)\norm{C}_2^2
    \notag\\
    &=
    (1-t)(1-rt)\norm{C}_2^2
    \ge0.
\label{eq:q2-final-bound}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Exact rank-constrained two-copy score curve]
\label[proposition]{prop:exact-q2-score}
Let $d\ge2$ and $1\le r\le d$.  For $t\ge0$, define
\[
    \mu_r(t)
    :=
    \inf_{\substack{C\in M_{d^2}(\C)\setminus\{0\}\\
                     \rank C\le r}}
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-t,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact-q2-score}
    \mu_r(t)
    =
    \begin{cases}
      (1-t)(1-rt),&0\le t\le 1/r,\\[1mm]
      1-rt,&t\ge1/r.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Both branches are attained by rank-$r$ matrices.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $C\ne0$ of rank at most $r$, set
\[
    z:=\frac{\abs{\Tr C}^2}{\norm{C}_2^2}.
\]
The trace--rank estimate gives $0\le z\le r$.  Dividing
\cref{eq:q2-first-bound} by $\norm{C}_2^2$ yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:q2-score-z}
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-t,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    \ge
    1-rt+\left(t^2-\frac tr\right)z.
\end{equation}
If $0\le t\le1/r$, the coefficient of $z$ is nonpositive, so
\cref{eq:q2-score-z} and $z\le r$ give
\[
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-t,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    \ge
    1-rt+r\left(t^2-\frac tr\right)
    =(1-t)(1-rt).
\]
Choose a rank-$r$ projection $P_r\in M_d(\C)$ and a unit vector
$v\in\C^d$.  For
\[
    C=P_r\otimes\ketbra{v}{v}
\]
one has
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \norm{C}_2^2&=r,
    &\abs{\Tr C}^2&=r^2,\\
    \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2&=r^2,
    &\norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2&=r,
\end{aligned}
\]
and hence equality holds.

If $t\ge1/r$, the coefficient of $z$ in
\cref{eq:q2-score-z} is nonnegative, so
\[
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-t,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    \ge1-rt.
\]
Choose orthogonal unit vectors $v,w\in\C^d$ and set
\[
    C=P_r\otimes\ketbra{v}{w}.
\]
Then
\[
    \Tr C=0,
    \qquad
    \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2=r^2,
    \qquad
    \Tr_2C=0,
\]
so equality again holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the range $r\le d$ is essential]
The extremizers in \cref{prop:exact-q2-score} require a rank-$r$
projection in one $d$-dimensional tensor factor.  The score formula does
not in general extend to $r>d$ by replacing $r$ with
$\min\{r,d\}$.  For example, when the rank bound is $r=d^2$, no rank
constraint remains.  If $0\le t\le1/d$, the smallest eigenvalue of
\[
    \left(\rho_{-t}^\Gamma\right)^{\otimes2}
\]
is
\[
    (1-dt)^2.
\]
By \cref{eq:q2-vectorization}, the displayed infimum is the smallest
eigenvalue, and therefore
\[
    \inf_{C\ne0}
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-t,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    =(1-dt)^2.
\]
This generally differs from $(1-t)(1-dt)$, the expression obtained by
formally substituting $d$ for $r$ in the first branch of
\cref{eq:exact-q2-score}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[An explicit adjacent-Schmidt-rank violation]
\label[corollary]{cor:adjacent-rank-gap}
Let $1\le r<d$ and put
\[
    Z_{r,d}
    :=
    \left(
      I-\frac1r\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}
    \right)^{\otimes2}.
\]
Across the regrouped bipartition $(A_1A_2):(B_1B_2)$,
\[
    \ip{\psi}{Z_{r,d}\psi}\ge0
    \qquad
    \text{whenever }\norm{\psi}=1
    \text{ and }\SR(\psi)\le r.
\]
On the other hand, there is a unit vector $\psi_{r+1}$ of Schmidt
rank $r+1$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adjacent-rank-negative-score}
    \ip{\psi_{r+1}}{Z_{r,d}\psi_{r+1}}
    =-\frac1r.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The nonnegativity assertion is
\cref{cor:two-copy-block-positive} at $t=1/r$.
Choose a rank-$(r+1)$ projection $P_{r+1}$ and orthogonal unit vectors
$v,w\in\C^d$, and define
\[
    C=P_{r+1}\otimes\ketbra{v}{w},
    \qquad
    \psi_{r+1}:=\frac{\operatorname{vec}(C)}{\sqrt{r+1}}.
\]
Then $\SR(\psi_{r+1})=\rank C=r+1$, while
\[
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-1/r,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    =1-\frac{r+1}{r}
    =-\frac1r.
\]
Now use \cref{eq:q2-vectorization}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A strict signed separation]
\label[remark]{rem:strict-rank-gap}
Let $0<\varepsilon<1/(r+1)$ and set
$t=(1-\varepsilon)/r$.  Then
\cref{prop:exact-q2-score} gives
\[
    \frac{q^{(2)}(-t,C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    \ge
    \varepsilon\left(1-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{r}\right)>0
    \qquad(\rank C\le r),
\]
whereas the rank-$(r+1)$ matrix used in
\cref{cor:adjacent-rank-gap} has normalized score
\[
    1-(r+1)t
    =\frac{(r+1)\varepsilon-1}{r}<0.
\]
Thus the filter has a uniform positive margin on all normalized
matrices of rank at most $r$, while the displayed rank-$(r+1)$ matrix
has negative score.
\end{remark}

This yields the exact $r$-block-positivity threshold for the
two-copy family $(\rho_\alpha^\Gamma)^{\otimes2}$.

\begin{corollary}[Two-copy block positivity]
\label[corollary]{cor:two-copy-block-positive}
Let $d\ge2$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb R$. For every $1\le r\le d^2$, the operator
\[
    (\rho_\alpha^\Gamma)^{\otimes2}
\]
is $r$-block-positive across the regrouped bipartition
\[
    (A_1A_2):(B_1B_2)
\]
if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:block-positive-threshold}
    \alpha
    \ge
    -\frac1{\min\{r,d\}}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
It remains only to consider $\alpha=-t<0$.

Suppose first that $r\le d$. If $t\le1/r$, then
\cref{eq:q2-final-bound} gives
\[
    q^{(2)}(-t,C)\ge0
\]
for every $C$ of rank at most $r$, proving $r$-block positivity.

For necessity in all cases, set
\[
    s:=\min\{r,d\}.
\]
Choose a rank-$s$ projection $P_s$ and orthogonal unit vectors
$v,w\in\C^d$, and let
\[
    C=P_s\otimes\ketbra{v}{w}.
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \rank C&=s\le r,\\
    \Tr C&=0,\\
    \norm{C}_2^2&=s,\\
    \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2&=s^2,\\
    \norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence
\[
    q^{(2)}(-t,C)
    =
    s(1-st),
\]
which is negative whenever $t>1/s$. Thus
$r$-block positivity forces
\[
    t\le\frac1{\min\{r,d\}}.
\]

Finally, suppose $r>d$. If $t\le1/d$, then the Choi operator of
\[
    \mathcal R_{-t}(X)
    :=
    \Tr(X)I-tX
\]
is
\[
    J(\mathcal R_{-t})
    =
    I-t\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}
    \succeq0,
\]
because $\norm{\Omega}^2=d$ and the only potentially nonpositive
eigenvalue is $1-td$. Therefore $\mathcal R_{-t}$ is completely
positive, so
$\mathcal R_{-t}^{\otimes2}$ is completely positive and hence
$r$-positive for every $r$. This proves sufficiency when $r>d$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$r$}{r}-positivity of the tensor square}

The preceding block-positivity statement has an equivalent map
formulation through the Choi--Jamiołkowski correspondence: a map is
$r$-positive if and only if its Choi operator is $r$-block-positive
\cite{Choi1975,TerhalHorodecki2000}. With the output factors grouped
against the input factors, the Choi operator of
$\mathcal R_\alpha^{\otimes2}$ is exactly
$(\rho_\alpha^\Gamma)^{\otimes2}$ across the bipartition
$(A_1A_2):(B_1B_2)$ used above.

\begin{corollary}[Tensor-square stability]
\label[corollary]{cor:tensor-square}
Let $d\ge2$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb R$. Define
\[
    \mathcal R_\alpha(X)
    :=
    \Tr(X)I+\alpha X.
\]
For every $1\le r\le d^2$, the tensor-square map
\[
    \mathcal R_\alpha^{\otimes2}
\]
is $r$-positive if and only if
\[
    \alpha
    \ge
    -\frac1{\min\{r,d\}}.
\]
\end{corollary}

The single factor is classical: $\mathcal R_\alpha$ on $M_d$ is $k$-positive
exactly when $\alpha\ge-1/k$, which is
\cite[Thm.~2(i)]{Tomiyama1985} after reparametrization, and
$\mathcal R_{-1/k}$ is the boundary map he denotes $\tau_k$.  The tensor square
does not follow from it, positivity being unstable under tensoring; the maps
whose positivity survives all of their own tensor powers are studied in
\cite{MullerHermesReebWolf2016}.  For $r\ge d$ the statement below is immediate,
each factor being then completely positive, so the content is the range $r<d$.

\subsection{Exact asymmetric tensor-product theorem}

For $a,b\ge0$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:q2-asymmetric}
    q_{a,b}(C)
    :=
    \norm{C}_2^2
    -a\norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2
    -b\norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2
    +ab\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\end{equation}
Under the same vectorization and regrouping as above,
\[
    q_{a,b}(C)
    =
    \left\langle
      \operatorname{vec}(C),
      \left(I-a\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}\right)
      \otimes
      \left(I-b\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}\right)
      \operatorname{vec}(C)
    \right\rangle.
\]

\begin{lemma}[One-sided rank bound]
\label[lemma]{lem:one-sided-partial-trace}
If $\rank C\le r$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one-sided-partial-trace}
    \norm{\Tr_jC}_2^2
    \le r\norm{C}_2^2,
    \qquad j\in\{1,2\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $C=\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\ketbra{e_i}{d_i}$ as in
\cref{eq:C-decomposition}, with $e_1,\ldots,e_{r_0}$ orthonormal and
$r_0=\rank C\le r$.  Identify a vector $x$ of the bipartite space with its
coefficient matrix $X$ relative to the chosen product basis, so that
$\norm{X}_2=\norm{x}$.  The two partial traces of a rank-one operator are then
matrix products,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ptrace-rank-one-product}
    \Tr_2\ketbra{x}{y}=XY^*,
    \qquad
    \Tr_1\ketbra{x}{y}=\overline{X^*Y},
\end{equation}
both immediate from the definitions.  The Hilbert--Schmidt norm is
submultiplicative, so each summand obeys
$\norm{\Tr_j\ketbra{e_i}{d_i}}_2\le\norm{e_i}\norm{d_i}=\norm{d_i}$.  By the
triangle inequality and then Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
    \norm{\Tr_jC}_2^2
    \le\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\norm{d_i}\Bigr)^2
    \le r_0\sum_{i=1}^{r_0}\norm{d_i}^2
    =r_0\norm{C}_2^2
    \le r\norm{C}_2^2,
\]
the equality being \cref{eq:C-decomposition}.
\end{proof}

Recall that $\Tr_1$ means the trace \emph{over} the first local
factor, leaving an operator on the second, while $\Tr_2$ traces over
the second and leaves an operator on the first.  Thus the coefficient
$a$ in \cref{eq:q2-asymmetric} multiplies the first-factor trace and
$b$ multiplies the second-factor trace.

\begin{theorem}[Exact asymmetric tensor-product score]
\label[theorem]{thm:exact-asymmetric-score}
Let $d\ge2$, $1\le r\le d$, and $a,b\ge0$.  Put
\[
    m:=\max\{a,b\},
    \qquad
    \ell:=\min\{a,b\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact-asymmetric-score}
    \inf_{\substack{C\in M_{d^2}(\C)\setminus\{0\}\\
                     \rank C\le r}}
    \frac{q_{a,b}(C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    =
    \begin{cases}
      (1-\ell)(1-rm),&m\le1/r,\\[1mm]
      1-rm,&m\ge1/r.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By interchanging the two tensor factors, assume $a=m$ and $b=\ell$.
For $C\ne0$ of rank at most $r$, write
\[
    x:=\frac{\norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2}{\norm{C}_2^2},
    \qquad
    y:=\frac{\norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2}{\norm{C}_2^2},
    \qquad
    z:=\frac{\abs{\Tr C}^2}{\norm{C}_2^2}.
\]
By \cref{thm:rank_r,lem:one-sided-partial-trace},
\[
    x+y\le r+\frac zr,
    \qquad
    x\le r,
    \qquad
    0\le z\le r.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
    ax+by
    &=b(x+y)+(a-b)x\\
    &\le b\left(r+\frac zr\right)+(a-b)r
      =ar+\frac br z,
\end{align*}
and hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asymmetric-z-bound}
    \frac{q_{a,b}(C)}{\norm{C}_2^2}
    \ge
    1-ar+b\left(a-\frac1r\right)z.
\end{equation}
If $a\le1/r$, minimize the right-hand side over $0\le z\le r$
by taking $z=r$; this gives
\[
    1-ar+br\left(a-\frac1r\right)
    =(1-b)(1-ar).
\]
Equality is attained by
$C=P_r\otimes\ketbra{v}{v}$.
If $a\ge1/r$, minimize by taking $z=0$; this gives $1-ar$, with
equality attained by
$C=P_r\otimes\ketbra{v}{w}$ for $v\perp w$.
If $b>a$, interchange the two tensor factors in these constructions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Asymmetric tensor-product $r$-positivity threshold]
\label[corollary]{cor:asymmetric-block-positive}
Let $d\ge2$, $1\le r\le d^2$, and $a,b\ge0$.  Then
\[
    \left(I-a\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}\right)
    \otimes
    \left(I-b\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}\right)
\]
is $r$-block-positive across the regrouped bipartition if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:asymmetric-threshold}
    \max\{a,b\}
    \le
    \frac1{\min\{r,d\}}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
    \mathcal R_{-a}\otimes\mathcal R_{-b}
\]
is $r$-positive if and only if \cref{eq:asymmetric-threshold} holds.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The corner $a=b=1/r$ of the sufficiency half is
\cref{rem:operator-ineq-as-map}, which derives it from
\cref{prop:operator_ineq} directly; it does not depend on this section.  For the
general case with $r\le d$, the assertion follows from
\cref{thm:exact-asymmetric-score}.  (That route passes through
\cref{thm:rank_r}, hence through \cref{prop:operator_ineq}; the remark shows the
$a=b=1/r$ corner without the detour.  The other corners do need an argument
rather than a citation: $\mathcal R_{-a}=\mathcal R_{-1/r}+(\frac1r-a)\id$, and
the cross terms $\mathcal R_{-1/r}\otimes\id$ are not separately positive, so
monotonicity in $a$ and $b$ is not immediate.)  If $r>d$ and
$a,b\le1/d$, both factors
$I-a\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}$ and
$I-b\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}$ are positive semidefinite, so their
tensor product is positive semidefinite.  Conversely, if, say,
$a>1/d$, choose
\[
    C=I_d\otimes\ketbra{v}{w},
    \qquad v\perp w.
\]
Then $\rank C=d\le r$ and
$q_{a,b}(C)/\norm{C}_2^2=1-ad<0$.  The case $b>1/d$ follows by
interchanging the tensor factors.  The map statement follows from the
Choi--Jamiołkowski correspondence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Quantitative trace-norm separation from bounded Schmidt number}

\begin{corollary}[Trace-norm separation certified by the two-copy witness]
\label[corollary]{cor:two-copy-trace-distance}
Let $1\le r<d$ and let $Z_{r,d}$ be as in
\cref{cor:adjacent-rank-gap}.  Put
\[
    \gamma:=\frac dr-1>0,
    \qquad
    \Delta_{r,d}:=\gamma+\max\{1,\gamma^2\}.
\]
Define the normalized Schmidt-number-$r$ state set
\[
    \mathcal S_r
    :=
    \left\{
      \sigma\succeq0:
      \Tr\sigma=1,\ \SN(\sigma)\le r
    \right\}.
\]
If $\rho$ is a state satisfying
\[
    \Tr(Z_{r,d}\rho)=-\varepsilon<0,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:two-copy-trace-distance}
    \inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal S_r}
    \norm{\rho-\sigma}_1
    \ge
    \frac{2\varepsilon}{\Delta_{r,d}}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $Z_{r,d}$ is $r$-block-positive,
\[
    \Tr(Z_{r,d}\sigma)\ge0
    \qquad
    \text{for every }\sigma\in\mathcal S_r.
\]
The operator
$I-r^{-1}\ketbra{\Omega}{\Omega}$ has eigenvalues $1$ and
$1-d/r=-\gamma$.  Hence $Z_{r,d}$ has eigenvalues
\[
    1,
    \qquad
    -\gamma,
    \qquad
    \gamma^2.
\]
Thus its spectral diameter is
$\Delta_{r,d}$.

Fix $\sigma\in\mathcal S_r$.  Then
\[
    \Tr\bigl[Z_{r,d}(\sigma-\rho)\bigr]\ge\varepsilon.
\]
Because $\Tr(\sigma-\rho)=0$, we may subtract from $Z_{r,d}$ the
midpoint of its extreme eigenvalues.  The resulting centered operator
has operator norm $\Delta_{r,d}/2$.  H\"older duality therefore gives
\[
    \varepsilon
    \le
    \frac{\Delta_{r,d}}2\norm{\sigma-\rho}_1.
\]
Taking the infimum over $\sigma\in\mathcal S_r$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Kronecker-sum Ky Fan bounds}

By dualizing the partial-trace inequality over rank-$k$ test
operators, \cref{thm:rank_r} yields a singular-value hierarchy for
Kronecker sums.

\begin{corollary}
\label[corollary]{cor:kronecker}
Let $A,B\in M_d(\C)$ be traceless, not necessarily Hermitian,
operators, and define
\[
    M
    :=
    A\otimes I_d+I_d\otimes B.
\]
For every $1\le k\le d^2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kronecker-bound}
    \sum_{j=1}^k s_j(M)^2
    \le
    \min\left\{
      d,
      k+\max\left\{
        0,1-\frac{2k}{d}
      \right\}
    \right\}
    \left(
      \norm{A}_2^2+\norm{B}_2^2
    \right).
\end{equation}
In particular, if $d\le2k$, then
\[
    \sum_{j=1}^k s_j(M)^2
    \le
    k\left(
      \norm{A}_2^2+\norm{B}_2^2
    \right).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Frobenius-dual form of Ky Fan duality gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ky-fan-duality}
    \left(
      \sum_{j=1}^k s_j(M)^2
    \right)^{1/2}
    =
    \max_{\substack{\rank C\le k\\ \norm{C}_2=1}}
    \abs{\langle C,M\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}}.
\end{equation}
Since $A$ and $B$ are traceless,
\begin{align}
    \langle C,M\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
    ={}&
    \left\langle
      \Tr_2C-\frac{\Tr C}{d}I,
      A
    \right\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}
    \notag\\
    &+
    \left\langle
      \Tr_1C-\frac{\Tr C}{d}I,
      B
    \right\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}.
\label{eq:kronecker-pairing}
\end{align}
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{align}
    \abs{\langle C,M\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}}^2
    \le{}&
    \left(
      \norm{A}_2^2+\norm{B}_2^2
    \right)
    \notag\\
    &\times
    \left(
      \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2
      +
      \norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2
      -
      \frac2d\abs{\Tr C}^2
    \right).
\label{eq:kronecker-CS}
\end{align}
For a rank-at-most-$k$ operator $C$ with $\norm{C}_2=1$,
\cref{eq:main-bound} gives
\begin{align}
    &
    \norm{\Tr_1C}_2^2
    +
    \norm{\Tr_2C}_2^2
    -
    \frac2d\abs{\Tr C}^2
    \notag\\
    &\qquad
    \le
    k
    +
    \left(
      \frac1k-\frac2d
    \right)\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\label{eq:kronecker-main-use}
\end{align}
If $1/k-2/d\le0$, the final term may be discarded. If
$1/k-2/d>0$, then
\[
    \abs{\Tr C}^2
    \le
    k\norm{C}_2^2
    =
    k.
\]
Thus the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:kronecker-main-use} is at most
\[
    k+\max\left\{
      0,1-\frac{2k}{d}
    \right\}.
\]
Combining this with
\cref{eq:ky-fan-duality,eq:kronecker-CS} gives the second entry in the
minimum in \cref{eq:kronecker-bound}. On the other hand, tracelessness
implies
\[
    \norm{M}_2^2
    =d\left(\norm{A}_2^2+\norm{B}_2^2\right),
\]
which gives the first entry. This proves \cref{eq:kronecker-bound}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Exact Schmidt number and witnesses}

For $m\ge2$, let $\Pi_-^{(m)}$ denote the orthogonal projection onto the
antisymmetric subspace of $\C^m\otimes\C^m$, and let
\[
    \omega_-^{(m)}
    :=
    \frac{\Pi_-^{(m)}}{\binom m2}
\]
denote the corresponding normalized state.

The argument below invokes \cref{lem:double-skew} directly rather than
\cref{thm:rank_r}: what it needs is the bound on the top two Schmidt
coefficients of a double-skew operator, not the partial-trace inequality
derived from it.  That $\SN(\omega_-^{(m)})=2$ is classical; the content
here is the value for the tensor product.

\begin{corollary}[Exact Schmidt number]
\label[corollary]{cor:exact-schmidt-number}
With respect to the regrouped bipartition
\[
    (A_1A_2):(B_1B_2),
\]
one has, for every $m,n\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SN-four}
    \SN\left(
      \omega_-^{(m)}\otimes\omega_-^{(n)}
    \right)
    =
    4.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each standard antisymmetric basis vector has Schmidt rank $2$, so
$\omega_-^{(m)}$ admits a decomposition into pure states of Schmidt
rank $2$. Moreover, the antisymmetric subspace contains no nonzero
product vector. Hence
\[
    \SN(\omega_-^{(m)})=2.
\]
Tensoring Schmidt-rank-$2$ decompositions therefore gives
\[
    \SN\left(
      \omega_-^{(m)}\otimes\omega_-^{(n)}
    \right)
    \le4.
\]

For the reverse inequality, let
\[
    Q
    :=
    \Pi_-^{(m)}\otimes \Pi_-^{(n)},
\]
where the first projection acts on $A_1B_1$ and the second on $A_2B_2$.
Under inverse vectorization across
$(A_1A_2):(B_1B_2)$, every vector
$\phi\in\ran Q$ has the form
\[
    \phi=\operatorname{vec}(K),
    \qquad
    K\in
    \mathfrak{so}(\C^m)\otimes\mathfrak{so}(\C^n).
\]
Thus, for a unit vector $\phi\in\ran Q$, its Schmidt coefficients
$s_1\ge s_2\ge s_3\ge\cdots$ across the regrouped bipartition are the
singular values of a Hilbert--Schmidt unit operator $K$ in the
above double-skew space. The case $m\ne n$ is included by the
zero-extension argument of \cref{rem:fgp-equivalence}.
By that lemma,
\[
    s_1^2+s_2^2\le\frac12.
\]
Since $s_3\le s_2\le s_1$,
\[
    2s_3^2
    \le
    s_1^2+s_2^2
    \le
    \frac12.
\]
Consequently,
\[
    s_1^2+s_2^2+s_3^2
    \le
    \frac34.
\]
For any orthogonal projection $Q$ and any $k$, projection duality gives
\begin{align*}
    \sup_{\substack{\norm{z}=1\\ \SR(z)\le k}}
    \ip{z}{Qz}
    &=
    \sup_{\substack{\phi\in\ran Q\\ \norm{\phi}=1}}
    \sup_{\substack{\norm{z}=1\\ \SR(z)\le k}}
    \abs{\ip{z}{\phi}}^2\\
    &=
    \sup_{\substack{\phi\in\ran Q\\ \norm{\phi}=1}}
    \sum_{j=1}^k s_j(\phi)^2;
\end{align*}
see \cite{JohnstonKribs2010}. Applying this identity with $k=3$ gives
\[
    \sup_{\substack{\norm{z}=1\\ \SR(z)\le3}}
    \ip{z}{Qz}
    \le
    \frac34.
\]

On the other hand,
\[
    \omega_-^{(m)}\otimes\omega_-^{(n)}
    =\frac{Q}{\rank Q},
\]
and hence
\[
    \Tr\left[
      Q\left(
        \omega_-^{(m)}\otimes\omega_-^{(n)}
      \right)
    \right]
    =1.
\]
If the state had Schmidt number at most $3$, it would admit a convex
decomposition into pure states of Schmidt rank at most $3$
\cite{TerhalHorodecki2000}; its expectation against $Q$ would then be at
most $3/4$, a contradiction. Therefore its Schmidt number is at least
$4$, proving \cref{eq:SN-four}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit Schmidt-number witnesses]
\label[corollary]{cor:explicit-witnesses}
Let
\[
    Q=\Pi_-^{(m)}\otimes \Pi_-^{(n)}.
\]
Then
\[
    W_2:=\frac12I-Q
\]
satisfies
\[
    \Tr(W_2\sigma)\ge0
    \qquad
    \text{whenever }\SN(\sigma)\le2,
\]
and
\[
    W_3:=\frac34I-Q
\]
satisfies
\[
    \Tr(W_3\sigma)\ge0
    \qquad
    \text{whenever }\SN(\sigma)\le3.
\]
Thus a negative expectation of $W_2$ certifies Schmidt number at
least $3$, while a negative expectation of $W_3$ certifies Schmidt
number at least $4$. For the state in
\cref{cor:exact-schmidt-number},
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Tr\left[
      W_2\left(
        \omega_-^{(m)}\otimes\omega_-^{(n)}
      \right)
    \right]
    &=-\frac12,\\
    \Tr\left[
      W_3\left(
        \omega_-^{(m)}\otimes\omega_-^{(n)}
      \right)
    \right]
    &=-\frac14.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Gram structure and defect identities}
\label{app:gram}

This section records the algebraic defect structure of the rank-$r$
proof. The main residual is represented by positive Gram operators
once the range isometry is fixed; this is not claimed to be a uniform
bounded-degree polynomial sum-of-squares identity in the raw Stiefel
coordinates. The only literal polynomial SOS below is the elementary
trace--rank residual.

For an operator $C$ of rank at most $r$, define
\begin{align}
    \mathsf G_r(C)
    &:=
    r\norm C_2^2+\frac1r\abs{\Tr C}^2
    -\norm{\Tr_U C}_2^2-\norm{\Tr_V C}_2^2,
\label{eq:sos-main-gap}\\
    \mathsf R_r(C)
    &:=
    r\norm C_2^2-\abs{\Tr C}^2.
\label{eq:sos-trace-rank-gap}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Polynomial SOS for the trace--rank residual]
\label[proposition]{prop:sos-trace-rank}
Assume $r\le\dim(U\otimes V)$ and write
\[
    C=\sum_{i=1}^r\ketbra{e_i}{d_i},
\]
where $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ are orthonormal and zero columns $d_i=0$ are
allowed. In $(U\otimes V)\otimes\C^r$, put
\[
    \mathbf e:=\sum_i e_i\otimes q_i,
    \qquad
    \mathbf d:=\sum_i d_i\otimes q_i.
\]
Equip the exterior square with the convention that
$u_\alpha\wedge u_\beta$ for $\alpha<\beta$ is orthonormal whenever
$(u_\alpha)$ is an orthonormal basis. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sos-lagrange}
    \mathsf R_r(C)
    =\norm{\mathbf e\wedge\mathbf d}^2.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, in any orthonormal coordinate system,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sos-lagrange-coordinates}
    \mathsf R_r(C)
    =
    \sum_{\alpha<\beta}
    \abs{
      \mathbf e_\alpha\mathbf d_\beta
      -\mathbf e_\beta\mathbf d_\alpha
    }^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One has
\[
    \norm{\mathbf e}^2=r,
    \qquad
    \norm{\mathbf d}^2=\norm C_2^2,
    \qquad
    \abs{\ip{\mathbf e}{\mathbf d}}=\abs{\Tr C}.
\]
The complex Lagrange identity gives both formulas.
\end{proof}

For the remainder of this section, fix an exact rank-$r$
factorization
\[
    C=\sum_{i=1}^r\ketbra{e_i}{d_i},
    \qquad
    \delta_e=\sum_i e_i\otimes q_i,
    \qquad
    \delta_d=\sum_i d_i\otimes q_i,
\]
and retain $\rho_0$, $\Pi_e$, $P_\pm$, and $\Phi$ from the proof of
\cref{prop:operator_ineq}. Let $\mathcal T_-:=\mathcal T$ be the
negative-sector synthesis map in \cref{eq:T-definition}. Choose an
orthonormal basis of $\ran P_+$ and let $\mathcal T_+$ be the
synthesis map whose columns are all Kraus images under $\Phi$ of those
positive-sector basis vectors. Then
\[
    \Phi(P_\pm)=\mathcal T_\pm\mathcal T_\pm^*.
\]

For a coefficient array $c=(c^{(ij)}_{\alpha\beta})$, define
\[
    K_{ij}:=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
    c^{(ij)}_{\alpha\beta}L_\alpha\otimes M_\beta.
\]
At a vertex $k$, associate to an incident edge the signed vectors
\[
    v_{k,ik}:=K_{ik}\overline e_i\quad(i<k),
    \qquad
    v_{k,kj}:=-K_{kj}\overline e_j\quad(k<j).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Complete-graph defect certificate]
\label[proposition]{prop:sos-complete-graph}
With the skew Kraus normalization used in \cref{eq:Kc-norm},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sos-complete-graph}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 &(r-1)\norm{c}_2^2-\norm{\mathcal T_-c}^2\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{r-1}{2}\sum_{i<j}
 \left[
   \frac12\norm{K_{ij}}_2^2
   -\norm{K_{ij}\overline e_i}^2
   -\norm{K_{ij}\overline e_j}^2
 \right]\\
 &\qquad+
 \frac12\sum_{k=1}^r
 \sum_{\substack{e<f\\e,f\ni k}}
 \norm{v_{k,e}-v_{k,f}}^2.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\norm{\mathcal T_-}^2\le r-1$ and
\[
    (r-1)(I-\Pi_e)-\mathcal T_-\mathcal T_-^*\succeq0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At every vertex use the exact variance identity
\[
    (r-1)\sum_{e\ni k}\norm{v_{k,e}}^2
    -\norm{\sum_{e\ni k}v_{k,e}}^2
    =
    \sum_{\substack{e<f\\e,f\ni k}}
    \norm{v_{k,e}-v_{k,f}}^2.
\]
By \cref{eq:T-by-vertices,eq:Kc-norm},
\begin{align*}
 &(r-1)\norm{c}_2^2-\norm{\mathcal T_-c}^2\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{r-1}{4}\sum_{i<j}\norm{K_{ij}}_2^2
 -\frac12\sum_{k=1}^r
   \norm{\sum_{e\ni k}v_{k,e}}^2\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{r-1}{4}\sum_{i<j}\norm{K_{ij}}_2^2
 -\frac{r-1}{2}\sum_{i<j}
   \left(
     \norm{K_{ij}\overline e_i}^2
     +\norm{K_{ij}\overline e_j}^2
   \right)\\
 &\qquad+
 \frac12\sum_{k=1}^r
 \sum_{\substack{e<f\\e,f\ni k}}
 \norm{v_{k,e}-v_{k,f}}^2.
\end{align*}
Factoring the first two sums gives exactly
\cref{eq:sos-complete-graph}.  The vertex-variance terms are
nonnegative, and each edge bracket is nonnegative by
\cref{lem:double-skew}. Hence $\norm{\mathcal T_-}^2\le r-1$.
Finally, \cref{eq:T-orthogonal} gives
$\ran\mathcal T_-\subseteq\delta_e^\perp$, whose projection is
$I-\Pi_e$, so the operator inequality follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact positive/negative-sector Gram identity]
\label[proposition]{prop:sos-sector-gram}
Put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sos-negative-defect}
    B_E
    :=(r-1)(I-\Pi_e)-\mathcal T_-\mathcal T_-^*.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sos-main-gram}
    \boxed{
    \mathsf G_r(C)
    =
    \norm{\mathcal T_+^*\delta_d}^2
    +\ip{\delta_d}{B_E\delta_d}.}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $B_E\succeq0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:partial-trace-contractions},
$\mathsf G_r(C)=\ip{\delta_d}{H_r^{(0)}\delta_d}$.  Substituting
\cref{eq:H-decomposition} and the two synthesis factorizations gives
\cref{eq:sos-main-gram}.  Positivity of $B_E$ is the operator
conclusion of \cref{prop:sos-complete-graph}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the Gram certificate]
The complete-graph identity separates the global synthesis-map defect
into vertex variances and edgewise double-skew deficits. Edgewise
nonnegativity is precisely the Fu--Gao--Park projection estimate used
in \cref{lem:double-skew}. No uniform polynomial SOS certificate in
the raw range-isometry variables is claimed.
\end{remark}


\section{Generic consequences of \texorpdfstring{$r$}{r}-block positivity}
\label{app:generic}

The two results collected here are separated from \cref{sec:applications}
because neither uses any property of the witnesses constructed there
beyond $r$-block positivity, and \cref{prop:local-witness-aggregation}
does not use \cref{thm:rank_r} at all.  They are recorded because they are
what makes those witnesses usable in practice.

\subsection{Matrix-valued Schmidt-number criteria}

\begin{corollary}[A positive-map semidefinite cut]
\label[corollary]{cor:positive-map-cut}
Let $1\le r\le d^2$, let $X$ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space,
and put
\[
    s:=\min\{r,d\},
    \qquad
    \Lambda_r
    :=
    \mathcal R_{-1/s}^{\otimes2}.
\]
If a positive semidefinite operator
$\sigma\in\cL\bigl(X\otimes(\C^d)^{\otimes2}\bigr)$ has Schmidt
number at most $r$ across
$X:(\C^d)^{\otimes2}$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-map-cut}
    (\id_X\otimes\Lambda_r)(\sigma)\succeq0.
\end{equation}
More generally, \cref{eq:positive-map-cut} holds with
$\Lambda_r$ replaced by
$\mathcal R_{-a}\otimes\mathcal R_{-b}$ whenever
$a,b\ge0$ and
\[
    \max\{a,b\}\le\frac1{\min\{r,d\}}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:asymmetric-block-positive}, the indicated map is
$r$-positive.  Decompose $\sigma$ into positive multiples of pure
states of Schmidt rank at most $r$.  For each such vector, its Schmidt
support on $X$ has dimension at most $r$, so positivity follows from
$r$-positivity after restricting the identity ancilla to that support.
Conjugating back into $X$ and summing the resulting positive operators
proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Use as an SDP cut]
Whenever a semidefinite relaxation contains an explicit candidate
state block $\sigma$, \cref{eq:positive-map-cut} is a linear matrix
inequality in the entries of that block.  If those entries are affine
functions of a moment matrix, applying $\id\otimes\Lambda_r$ produces
an affine matrix pencil and therefore a valid additional semidefinite
constraint under a Schmidt-number-$r$ promise.  This is a necessary
condition for that promise, not in general a complete semidefinite
characterization of Schmidt number at most $r$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Local aggregation of rank witnesses}

\begin{proposition}[Local-channel aggregation]
\label[proposition]{prop:local-witness-aggregation}
Let $\mathcal H_A$ and $\mathcal H_B$ be finite-dimensional Hilbert
spaces.  For each $e$ in a finite set $E$, let
\[
    \Phi_e^A:\cL(\mathcal H_A)\to\cL(\mathcal K_{A,e}),
    \qquad
    \Phi_e^B:\cL(\mathcal H_B)\to\cL(\mathcal K_{B,e})
\]
be quantum channels, let $W_e=W_e^*\in\cL(\mathcal K_{A,e}\otimes
\mathcal K_{B,e})$ be $r$-block-positive, and let
$\lambda_e\ge0$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:aggregated-witness}
    H
    :=
    \sum_{e\in E}
    \lambda_e
    (\Phi_e^A\otimes\Phi_e^B)^*(W_e)
\end{equation}
is $r$-block-positive across $\mathcal H_A:\mathcal H_B$.  In
particular,
\[
    \Tr(H\rho)\ge0
    \qquad
    \text{whenever }\SN(\rho)\le r.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Local quantum channels do not increase Schmidt number.  Indeed, write
\[
    \rho=\sum_k p_k\ketbra{\psi_k}{\psi_k},
    \qquad
    \SR(\psi_k)\le r,
\]
and choose Kraus operators $A_{e,i}$ and $B_{e,j}$ for
$\Phi_e^A$ and $\Phi_e^B$.  Then
\[
    (\Phi_e^A\otimes\Phi_e^B)(\rho)
    =
    \sum_{k,i,j}p_k
    \ketbra{(A_{e,i}\otimes B_{e,j})\psi_k}
            {(A_{e,i}\otimes B_{e,j})\psi_k},
\]
and every vector in this decomposition has Schmidt rank at most $r$.
Hence
\[
    \SN\bigl((\Phi_e^A\otimes\Phi_e^B)(\rho)\bigr)\le r
\]
for every $e$.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \Tr(H\rho)
    &=
    \sum_{e\in E}\lambda_e
    \Tr\left[
      W_e(\Phi_e^A\otimes\Phi_e^B)(\rho)
    \right]\\
    &\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A local-Hamiltonian Schmidt-number certificate]
\label[corollary]{cor:local-hamiltonian-rank-certificate}
In \cref{prop:local-witness-aggregation}, suppose each local output
contains two matched $d_e$-dimensional pairs, with $r\le d_e$, and take
\[
    W_e
    =
    \left(
      I-\frac1r\ketbra{\Omega_e}{\Omega_e}
    \right)^{\otimes2}.
\]
Then the operator $H$ in \cref{eq:aggregated-witness} satisfies
\[
    \Tr(H\rho)<0
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \SN(\rho)\ge r+1.
\]
For literal subsystem tests, the channels $\Phi_e^A$ and $\Phi_e^B$
may be taken to be the corresponding partial traces, so $H$ is a sum
of local terms embedded into the global Hilbert space.  More generally,
any geometric locality of $H$ is inherited from the subsystem supports
of the chosen channels; arbitrary channels need not produce local
Hamiltonian terms.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Positive-semidefinite pulled-back terms]
Write
\[
    \Psi_e:=\Phi_e^A\otimes\Phi_e^B
\]
and shift the output-space witness to
\[
    h_e^{\mathrm{out}}
    :=
    W_e-\lambda_{\min}(W_e)I_e
    \succeq0,
\]
where $I_e$ is the identity on
$\mathcal K_{A,e}\otimes\mathcal K_{B,e}$.  Its global pullback
\[
    \widetilde h_e
    :=
    \Psi_e^*(h_e^{\mathrm{out}})
    \succeq0
\]
is well defined even when the output spaces depend on $e$.  Since
$\Psi_e$ is trace-preserving, $\Psi_e^*(I_e)=I$, and hence
\[
    \sum_e\lambda_e\widetilde h_e
    =
    H-
    \left(
      \sum_e\lambda_e\lambda_{\min}(W_e)
    \right)I.
\]
Consequently, every normalized $\rho$ with $\SN(\rho)\le r$ satisfies
\[
    \Tr\left[
      \sum_e\lambda_e\widetilde h_e\,\rho
    \right]
    \ge
    -\sum_e\lambda_e\lambda_{\min}(W_e).
\]
For the witnesses in
\cref{cor:local-hamiltonian-rank-certificate},
\[
    \lambda_{\min}(W_e)=1-\frac{d_e}{r},
\]
so the known energy baseline is
\[
    \sum_e\lambda_e\left(\frac{d_e}{r}-1\right).
\]
This construction certifies Schmidt number across the prescribed
bipartition; by itself it is not an NLTS statement.
\end{remark}
